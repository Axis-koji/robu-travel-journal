Hello, I'm Robu.

This time, I wasn't heading out just to eat yatsuhashi, Kyoto's traditional sweets. I fancied a change of scenery and somewhere for lunch, so I drove to Izutsu Yatsuhashi Honpo's Shinkouetsu store in Nantan, Kyoto Prefecture.

The shop is known for its Yuko sweets, but our visit began with curry and hayashi rice in the café, followed by a relaxing matcha break. Afterwards, we browsed the shop for souvenirs.

A leisurely drive from Osaka to Nantan

From my starting point on this particular trip, the estimated journey was about an hour whether I took the expressway or ordinary roads. I decided to take the ordinary roads and enjoy a relaxed drive. The journey time will depend on where in Osaka you start and the traffic that day.

Our destination was the Shinkouetsu store, which the official website describes as a shop with an adjoining production facility. We were offered yatsuhashi when we entered—a lovely start. But lunch came first, so we headed straight to the café at the back.

Curry and hayashi rice at a yatsuhashi shop

Here is what we chose for lunch. It was nice to discover that this sweets shop offered meals too.

Curry rice

The curry had a good, bold spiciness. I enjoyed the meat, and there were finely chopped ingredients in the sauce as well.

It wasn't a mild, sweet curry; to me, the heat felt about medium. I thought it struck a pleasant balance for adults who enjoy some spice without wanting an overly hot meal.

Hayashi rice

A small detail made me smile: the cutlery basket held an individually wrapped Izutsu Yatsuhashi biscuit alongside the spoons and hand towels.

I was happy to find a whole biscuit included with lunch. It was a thoughtful little touch, and a reminder that we really were eating at a yatsuhashi shop.

A matcha break with soft yatsuhashi

After lunch, we ordered a matcha set. We enjoyed the tea's lovely aroma alongside yellow peach and Shine Muscat nama yatsuhashi—the soft, unbaked variety.

I usually picture the classic bean-paste-filled sweets when I think of nama yatsuhashi, but there are fruit flavours to explore too. It was nice to take our time over both a meal and something sweet in the same place.

Browsing seasonal Yuko sweets

After eating, we looked around the shop. Yellow peach, green apple and Shine Muscat varieties were on display during our visit, and some products were available to sample. There was a tea dispenser nearby too, so we could try a few things before deciding what to buy.

Although I didn't photograph it, the Milky flavour caught my interest as well. I couldn't sample it this time, but I had bought it on a previous occasion. Exploring these different flavours, not just the classics, is part of the fun of choosing souvenirs.

Kamo Sablé cookies with a cute duck illustration

These are Kamo Sablé cookies. The cute duck illustration caught my eye. I had come to browse yatsuhashi, but as I walked around, the other sweets were tempting too.

The products, prices shown in the photos, samples and meals reflect our visit. They may change with the season and availability.
