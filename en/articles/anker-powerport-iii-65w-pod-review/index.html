A closer look at my Anker PowerPort III 3-Port 65W Pod

Hello, I'm Robu.

After the power banks and cable, here is the charger that plugs into a wall outlet. This is my Anker PowerPort III 3-Port 65W Pod, identified by the A2667 model number on the body.

I use this charger for both my large 25,000mAh power bank and the small Anker Nano Power Bank I introduced earlier. It is an essential part of keeping my portable batteries ready to use.

Charging both my large and small power banks

My 25,000mAh power bank is for trips and other occasions when I need more capacity, while the small one stays in my everyday bag. They serve different purposes, but I use this adapter to recharge both.

After introducing the power banks themselves, it makes sense to show what I use to charge them. This is the charger that fills that role in my setup.

A video look at the charger

Two USB-C ports and one USB-A port

The two USB-C sockets sit above one USB-A socket. Having USB-A available alongside the two USB-C ports makes this combination very convenient for me.

Anker lists dimensions of approximately 38 × 29 × 66mm and a weight of about 130g. The plug folds away. Unlike a power bank, this charger needs a mains outlet and does not store power. Official specifications

Output depends on which ports are in use

A single USB-C port provides up to 65W. Using both USB-C ports divides the available output into up to 45W and 20W. With all three ports in use, the allocation is up to 40W, 12W and 12W, starting from the upper USB-C port.

It can also charge laptops that support USB-C PD and whose power requirements are compatible with its output. Port choice matters when charging a laptop alongside other devices. Actual charging speed also depends on the connected equipment and cables.

Charging feels fast in everyday use

In my own use, charging feels very fast. Since I regularly use it to recharge my power banks, that is another part of the experience I appreciate.

The "IQ" on this model refers to Anker's proprietary PowerIQ 3.0 (Gen2) charging technology, which supports charging standards including USB PD and Quick Charge. Anker's official feature description

65W is enough for my needs right now

Higher-output chargers are available, but I feel that 65W is enough for how I use mine at the moment. Rather than chasing the highest wattage, I chose this model for its balance of output and price.

Choosing a cable to go with the charger

The Anker right-angle USB-C cable I introduced earlier is one I use when I need more reach from a power source. Cable length and connector direction solve a different problem from the number of ports on a charger.

Cables are sold separately. Anker Japan's official store listed this charger at ¥5,490 including tax on October 10, 2026.

Robu's Take

I use this charger to recharge both my large 25,000mAh power bank and the small one I carry every day. The combination of two USB-C ports and one USB-A port makes it an essential part of my setup. For my current needs, 65W is enough, and I am happy with the balance of output and price.
