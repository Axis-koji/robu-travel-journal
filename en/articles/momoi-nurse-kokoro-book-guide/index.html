Momoi Nurse Looks After Hearts Too: 7 Checks Before Buying

A home-visit nurse can see the feelings of the people who live in the homes she visits. That's the premise that made me want to read Akiya Rinko's "Momoi Nurse Looks After Hearts Too" (Asahi Bunko).

Caregiving and nursing are subjects that most of us may touch someday. Yet reading about them head-on can feel heavy, and it's easy to hold back. In fiction, you can keep some distance while still finding a way to think about your own family. This guide uses the publisher's description and the author's biography to break the book into seven items to check before buying.

2. About the Author, Akiya Rinko

Akiya Rinko graduated from the Yokohama City University Junior College of Nursing (now the School of Nursing in the Faculty of Medicine) and then worked as a nurse on hospital wards for more than ten years.

In 2023, her story "Nurse Uzuki Sees Things" won the Bessatsu Bungeishunju Prize in note's Creative Writing Contest 2023. She made her debut in 2024 with the same work.

A story written by someone who has long worked in nursing is one possible reason to pick this book. I'm looking forward to seeing how her experience comes through in the writing.

3. The Subject: What the Publisher's Description Tells Us

According to the publisher's description, the protagonist is Momoi Yoshino, a home-visit nurse who can see people's hearts appear in their homes.

In this book, Momoi is assigned to Mrs. Hotta, who has dementia. She faces the thoughts hidden behind her husband's forgetfulness and the way furniture appears to vanish from view.

What drew me is the idea that hearts show up in the home. Home-visit nursing means entering the place where a person lives, not a hospital. A home reveals how a person lives and how a family relates. Adding the special ability to "see hearts" to that setting seems likely to give a caregiving and nursing story its own angle. This is only my imagination from the description.

I will keep spoilers within what the publisher's description gives. What happens beyond that is best left to the reader.

4. The Structure Visible in the Chapters

The table of contents lists five chapters and an afterword.

  • "Tomorrow for the Two of Us"
  • "Wavering Feelings"
  • "A Little Longer, Beside You"
  • "So They Can Be Themselves"
  • "Spring When Hearts Open"
  • Afterword

Even the chapter titles alone use words like "two," "beside," and "themselves," which suggest the distance between people. The last chapter, "Spring When Hearts Open," brings the turn of seasons to mind.

I'm looking forward to turning the pages and discovering where each title fits into the story.

5. Series Order: Start With Book One, or Jump In Here?

This book is the second in the "Momoi Nurse" series. The first is "Momoi Nurse Comes to Visit" (Momoi Nurse ga Ojama Shimasu).

Start with "Momoi Nurse Comes to Visit" if you'd like to get to know Momoi from the beginning. If this story about caring for family is what draws you in, you can pick up this volume first. Akiya herself says readers can start here.

6. A Book That Makes Me Think About Family

Sometimes the people closest to us are the hardest to read. When I learned that Momoi visits people at home and tries to understand what they're going through, I wondered how I'd feel and what I'd say if it were my own family. I'm also curious to see how Akiya draws on her years in nursing to write those encounters.
