Should You Buy the Dyson CameraJet? Seven Checks for the ¥79,800 Camera Toothbrush

The Dyson CameraJet combines an electric toothbrush, a close-range oral camera, and a jet that targets gaps with mouthrinse. Dyson's Japanese store displayed a price of ¥79,800 on October 14, 2026. The Ceramic Pink page was marked out of stock when checked.

That price places it far beyond an ordinary powered toothbrush. The decision depends less on headline technology than on whether you need visual guidance, can maintain the liquid system every day, and understand the limits of the supporting tests. Here are seven checks based on Dyson's official material.

1. Price and availability: ¥79,800, with changing stock

The Japanese product page listed a tax-inclusive price of ¥79,800. At the time of review, the Ceramic Pink version was out of stock and offered a restock-notification form. Inventory can differ by colour and seller, so an editor or buyer should check the official store again.

There is a useful timeline warning here. Dyson's Japanese launch announcement dated September 1 said that a Japanese release was undecided. The current product page subsequently added a yen price and stock status. For buying information, the live product page should take priority over an older announcement, while the difference should remain documented rather than silently ignored.

2. A 100k-pixel camera finds gaps

CameraJet's defining component is a 100,000-pixel macro camera. The Japanese page says it analyses 28 frames per second. When the system detects a gap, a conical jet delivers mouthrinse to that location. The camera also supports a live view in the app.

One hundred thousand pixels sounds small beside a phone camera, but this is a short-range detector rather than a camera for conventional photographs. The meaningful question is whether it detects gaps reliably in daily use, where angle, movement, toothpaste, and foam can affect the view.

Dyson recommends an SLS-free gel or non-foaming toothpaste and a clear, low-foaming mouthrinse. Excess foam may obscure the camera, and users are instructed to spit it out as needed. Anyone with a strong preference for an existing foaming product should include that compatibility issue in the purchase decision.

3. Brush and jet can operate separately

There are three cleaning configurations: brush plus auto-jet, auto-jet only, and brush only. Brushing intensity also has three levels—Sensitive, Daily Clean, and Deep Clean.

The ability to separate the functions is useful. It also prevents the jet from being treated as mandatory in every situation. For people with orthodontic appliances, Dyson's FAQ says to use brush-only mode, clean between the teeth with manual floss afterwards, and follow the orthodontist's instructions. The product should not be assumed to replace professional advice or every form of interdental cleaning.

4. The dock stores, charges, and refills

The included dock combines storage, charging, and mouthrinse refilling. Dyson's support page says its 200 ml reservoir can hold up to seven days of mouthrinse when brushing and flossing twice a day. The removable tank in the handpiece holds 12.5 ml.

This integration may reduce the number of steps before brushing, but liquid paths also create maintenance. Dyson instructs users to clean the product after every use: remove and wash the brush head and tank, hold the jet button until liquid stops flowing, rinse the body, and allow the parts to air-dry.

A fair time-saving comparison therefore includes both the cleaning session and the post-use routine. Someone who dislikes washing small wet components may find the integrated system less convenient than it first appears.

5. The app and oral video: Dyson says images are not stored

CameraJet supports 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and the MyDyson app. Official material describes live oral video, cleaning guidance, a coverage map, and reports showing cleaning history or trends. Features can vary by country, operating system, and device.

On privacy, Dyson says live images stream directly to the app, are not stored by Dyson or in the app, and are not uploaded to the cloud. A white light indicates that the camera is active, and the camera is described as off when a camera-dependent feature is not being used.

Those are the manufacturer's current statements, not a substitute for reading the current privacy notice and checking app permissions after updates. Buyers who do not want live oral video should ask which functions remain useful without connecting the app.

6. A 230 g body, up to ten days, and IPX7

The Japanese specification lists a 230 g handpiece measuring 44 by 44 by 273 mm, USB-C charging, a replaceable battery, and IPX7 water resistance. Dyson explains IPX7 as resistance to immersion at a depth of one metre for up to 30 minutes.

The advertised maximum battery period is ten days, but the usage condition matters. Dyson bases it on one brush-only session and one brush-plus-auto-jet session in Sensitive mode per day. Elsewhere the product page describes roughly 14 cleaning sessions. Stronger modes, different routines, or frequent live-video use may not reproduce the same calendar duration.

At 230 g, this is not a light manual brush. Grip comfort, weight at the mouth, bathroom space for the dock, and whether the dock and case will travel with you deserve more attention than the headline battery figure alone.

7. Read “more plaque removed” with the test conditions

Dyson's product page presents comparisons with manual brushing and premium electric toothbrushes. The conditions differ across claims. A four-week clinical study involved 71 participants using Dyson toothpaste and mouthrinse that, according to the footnote, were not sold in Japan as of September 2026. Some other comparisons used artificial teeth in laboratory tests.

These results should not be converted into a guarantee of the same outcome in every home. Dyson's FAQ tells people with gum disease, continuing dental treatment, sensitive teeth or gums, or other oral-health concerns to consult a dental professional before use. CameraJet is not a diagnostic device and does not replace dental examinations.

Who is it for?

CameraJet makes the most sense for a person who regularly forgets interdental care, values visual coverage feedback, and is willing to refill, rinse, dry, and manage an app as parts of one routine. It is also for someone who sees genuine value in brushing and targeted liquid flossing within a single coordinated system.

It is harder to justify for someone already consistent with a good electric toothbrush and manual floss, someone who dislikes cleaning the tank after every use, a person who would not use the camera or app, or a buyer focused on keeping replacement-head and consumable costs low.

Robu’s Take

CameraJet is more than a premium toothbrush with a camera attached. It is a complete oral-care system built around gap detection, a liquid jet, brushing, a refill dock, and app-based review. That is why lifestyle fit matters more than any one specification.

Before spending ¥79,800, ask three questions. Are you comfortable using a non-foaming toothpaste and low-foaming mouthrinse? Will you clean the liquid path after every use? Do you actually want to inspect live oral images and coverage reports? Three yes answers make the distinctive hardware easier to justify. If one or more answers are no, compare it with a simpler powered brush plus manual interdental care.
