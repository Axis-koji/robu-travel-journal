Should You Buy HomePod mini 2? Seven Checks Before Spending ¥24,800

Hi, I'm Roboo.

This is not a hands-on review. It is a pre-purchase guide based on Apple's official information.

Apple announced the next-generation HomePod mini, called HomePod mini 2, on October 13, 2026. Preorders are open through the Apple Store, and release is scheduled for October 30. The Japanese price is ¥24,800 including tax, with pink, green, blue, white and midnight finishes.[1][2] Price and availability are correct only as of this review; check Apple's current store page before buying.

¥24,800 is not a trivial amount for a small speaker. The more important questions are not simply how capable the unit looks, but which features will work in your own setup and what the four-speaker surround system really costs. Here are seven points worth checking first.

1. Price, release date, colors and what comes in the box

The basics are straightforward: Apple announced HomePod mini 2 on October 13, preorders are open, and the product is due on October 30. The Japanese price is ¥24,800 including tax, and the five colors are pink, green, blue, white and midnight.[1][2]

Availability can change, especially by finish, so confirm the delivery estimate for the color you want. Apple lists the speaker, a 20W USB-C power adapter and documentation in the box.[2] You should therefore be able to start with the supplied adapter rather than budgeting for one separately.

2. The S10 chip and computational audio

The HomePod mini 2 is built around Apple's S10 chip. Apple says it enables computational audio designed to deliver a richer listening experience.[1] In plain language, the speaker uses real-time processing to tune its output within the limits of a small enclosure.

That makes sense as a way to compensate for the physical limits of a compact speaker, and the S10 is central to that design. If sound quality is your top priority, check measurements and hands-on listening reports after release, or audition the speaker in a store with music you know well.

3. Driver design, 360-degree sound and an 84.3mm body

Apple describes a full-range driver, dual force-cancelling passive radiators and sound distributed around the speaker. The body is 84.3mm high.[1][4] Those specifications suggest a compact unit that can fit on a desk, shelf or kitchen counter.

The combination clearly aims to add low-end presence without giving up the small footprint. At 84.3mm high, it should be easy to place on a desk, kitchen counter or bedside table. The 360-degree design also makes it a practical choice when there is no obvious front-facing position for a speaker.

4. Stereo pairs, multiroom audio and the four-speaker surround conditions

HomePod mini 2 supports stereo pairing and multiroom playback.[1][4] Two matching speakers can form left and right channels, while multiple rooms can share audio.

The four-speaker surround option needs more explanation. Buying four speakers is not the only requirement. Apple's announcement specifies an Apple TV 4K (4th generation) with current operating software, and matching models within the front and rear pairs.[1] The front and rear pairs may use different models, but an all-HomePod mini 2 setup requires four mini 2 units.

An all-HomePod mini 2 setup costs ¥99,200 before the separately sold Apple TV 4K. That is a very different purchase from trying one speaker. A staged approach—one unit first, then a stereo pair—may be more practical unless you already know you want the complete setup.

I would start with one unit, or preferably a stereo pair, and decide on surround only after hearing what that setup adds. Buying all four units from the start is difficult to recommend at this stage.

5. Mac speaker mode, Sound Glow and Apple Intelligence requirements

Several ecosystem features have hardware or software conditions.

Using HomePod mini 2 as a Mac speaker, either alone or as a stereo pair, requires a Mac that supports Wi-Fi 6E or later.[1] Check your exact Mac model before treating this as a core reason to buy.

Voice control of Sound Glow requires an iPhone with current software and Apple Intelligence enabled.[1] Owning the speaker alone does not guarantee that every advertised interaction will be available. Your iPhone model, software version and settings remain part of the experience.

6. Smart-home hub, Sound Recognition, sensors and privacy

Apple positions HomePod mini 2 as a smart-home hub with Sound Recognition and temperature and humidity sensors.[1][3][4] These functions may be useful for automations and basic room awareness.

The safety limitation is essential. Apple warns that recognition of smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm sounds must not be relied on in an emergency.[1][4] It is an auxiliary notification feature, not a replacement for certified smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms.

Apple also says Siri audio is not stored by default.[1][3] That is a meaningful privacy statement, but it is not a blanket guarantee for every connected service or configuration. Because the speaker may sit in a living room or bedroom, review its privacy and sharing settings after setup rather than assuming the defaults match your preferences.

7. How it differs from the larger HomePod, plus the Apple Music trial

Apple's larger HomePod is priced at ¥59,800 and lists a high-excursion woofer, five tweeters, Spatial Audio and room sensing.[3][4] Those features are not listed in the same way for HomePod mini 2, so the smaller model should not be treated as a cheaper equivalent. They are different speakers for different spaces and priorities.

Apple also promotes a three-month Apple Music trial. Trial eligibility, renewal and billing conditions can depend on the account and offer. Confirm the terms shown during purchase or activation, including whether the subscription will renew at a paid rate.

Robu’s Take

HomePod mini 2 is a ¥24,800 compact smart speaker scheduled for October 30, 2026. Its official feature set combines the S10 chip, computational audio, 360-degree sound, stereo and multiroom options, and smart-home functions.[1][2][4]

My buying view is straightforward:

  • One unit is already appealing, especially for Mac users and people building an Apple Home setup.
  • A stereo pair is the most sensible next step.
  • An all-HomePod mini 2 four-speaker system should wait: the four speakers cost ¥99,200, and Apple TV 4K is extra.
  • Mac speaker mode and Sound Glow are useful only if your Mac and iPhone meet the stated requirements.
  • Sound Recognition must not replace dedicated smoke or carbon-monoxide alarms.
  • Do not treat the mini 2 as a lower-priced equivalent of the ¥59,800 HomePod.
  • Check the Apple Music trial's eligibility and renewal terms before activating it.

Before ordering, decide whether your Mac and iPhone support the features you want, where the speaker will sit, and whether one unit or a pair makes more sense. Once those three points are clear, it becomes much easier to judge whether ¥24,800 is money well spent.
