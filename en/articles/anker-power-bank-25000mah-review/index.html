My Seventh Anker Power Bank May Be My Favorite Yet

Hello, I'm Robu.

I use several Anker power banks for different situations, and this is probably the seventh one I have bought.

The Anker Power Bank (25000mAh, Built-In & Retractable USB-C Cables) has quickly become one of my favorites. Its display shows the remaining charge, two USB-C cables are built into the body, and its capacity and output are high enough for both a smartphone and a USB-C laptop.

It is compact for a 25,000mAh model, but at about 595g it still feels heavy in a bag. This is my firsthand account of where it works well, including my own charging test and a mistake I initially made about airline rules.

A display that makes the battery easier to manage

The display is one of the most useful features. It shows the remaining battery level as well as real-time input and output information. Being able to see the numbers gives me more confidence when I am away from home.

Two built-in cables reduce what I need to carry

The power bank has one retractable USB-C cable and one fixed USB-C cable that doubles as a strap. It also has one USB-C port and one USB-A port, allowing up to four devices to charge at once.

I still prefer to hold the body itself because the power bank is heavy, but the strap is useful for lightly hooking it onto something.

Enough capacity and output for a phone and a laptop

The battery capacity is 25,000mAh. A single USB-C connection supports up to 100W, while total output can reach 165W.

Anker says the power bank can charge an iPhone 17 about four times, but the actual number depends on the phone, its battery size and how it is used during charging.

With my FCNT arrows F-51E, charging the phone from 3 percent to 87 percent used about 34 percent of the power bank. In my own use, that suggests roughly three full phone charges.

This does not contradict Anker's estimate because the phones being compared are different. The most reliable estimate for your own phone comes from using it yourself.

Compact, but not light

The body measures about 158 × 54 × 49mm, so it is fairly compact for its capacity. The weight is about 595g, however, and I notice it once it is in my bag.

For me, this makes more sense for long days out, business trips, travel, or days when I carry a laptop than for every short trip.

Do not confuse 165W with the Wh figure used for airline rules

At first, I saw “165W” and mistakenly thought the product might exceed airline battery limits. The 165W figure describes maximum charging output. Airlines generally use watt-hours, or Wh, when determining how a portable battery may be carried. The figures describe different things.

I confirmed the rules when flying with JAL and Vietnam Airlines. Both airlines say portable batteries must travel in carry-on baggage rather than checked baggage. Limits and conditions can change, so check the operating airline's latest policy before travelling.

Robu's Take

The large capacity and high output are impressive, but the everyday convenience is what makes this power bank work for me. I can see the remaining charge, I do not need a separate cable, and I can use it for both my phone and laptop.

It is not the model I would choose when weight is the main concern. For longer days and trips, though, it has become my reliable high-capacity option.
