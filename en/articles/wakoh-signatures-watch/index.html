Before Choosing Wakoh's SIGNATURE(S): Japanese Movements, Domestic Final Assembly, and the Details to Verify

Hello, this is Roboo.

Wakoh, a watch manufacturer based in Toda, Saitama, has announced a new men's watch brand called SIGNATURE(S), scheduled to go on sale through its online store on October 2, 2026. The initial range consists of diver and chronograph designs, with two models and two colors in each group for a total of eight items.

The production disclosure is the most interesting part of the announcement. Wakoh states that the case and other components come from China, while assembly, case-back laser marking, final processing, and quality control take place in Japan. This Robu's Selection article therefore looks at both the attraction and the points that should be checked before purchase.

A new brand from a company founded in 1964

Wakoh's official website says the company began as a case supplier to major watchmakers and later applied its production and quality-control experience to its own brands. The company handles analog watches, smartwatches, commemorative products, and OEM work, and it also operates a repair center.

SIGNATURE(S) uses the phrase “The Right & Fine Manufacturing.” The brand says it wants to protect quality at an appropriate production scale rather than compete through mass production. Its stated goal is to create an everyday enthusiast's watch between utility-focused smartwatches and expensive mechanical pieces.

Reading the production description carefully

The press release describes production as “Made in JAPAN & International manufacturing.” It identifies China as the source of the case and component parts, and Japan as the location for assembly, laser marking, final processing, and quality control.

That is not the same as saying that every component is manufactured in Japan. It does, however, clearly state that the movement is Japanese and that final assembly and quality processes are carried out in Japan. Buyers should understand this division instead of relying only on a short “Made in Japan” label.

Who may prefer each model

MODEL:D is for someone who enjoys a mechanical movement and diver styling. Its announced specifications include 20-bar water resistance, a unidirectional bezel, and a screw-lock crown. Actual diving use must still follow the conditions in the official instruction manual, and a mechanical movement requires an understanding of rate variation and periodic service.

MODEL:C may suit someone who prefers quartz convenience while enjoying chronograph styling. It includes a 60-minute counter and 10-bar water resistance. The intended use of its bidirectional bezel and the chronograph controls should be confirmed in the official documentation.

Five details to verify before buying

  1. Case diameter, thickness, weight, and lug width.
  2. Crystal material, lume, magnetic resistance, and accuracy tolerances.
  3. Warranty length, repair support, and estimated service costs.
  4. Strap dimensions and supported wrist sizes.
  5. Stock, shipping date, and return conditions when sales open.

Roboo's conclusion

SIGNATURE(S) is notable for combining Japanese movements, final assembly and quality control in Japan, and original exterior components. The prices—JPY 71,500 for the diver and JPY 66,000 for the chronograph—are significant for an everyday watch, but the range may interest buyers seeking a long-term object outside both the smartwatch and luxury-mechanical categories.

The production is not entirely domestic, and important details such as dimensions and warranty terms still need confirmation. The sensible choice is to review the complete product and support information when sales begin on October 2.
