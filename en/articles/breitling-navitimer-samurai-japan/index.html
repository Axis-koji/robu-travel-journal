That indigo looks fantastic: the Navitimer GMT 41 Samurai Japan

This time, a Breitling watch caught my attention: the Navitimer Automatic GMT 41 Samurai Japan Limited.

The first thing that catches my eye is the indigo.

Looking at the pictures, I imagine its appearance could change with the light. Then there is that single crimson GMT hand. I like that the Japanese character is expressed without being overstated. It looks rather good.

Limited to 100 pieces in Japan. The leather-strap version is ¥924,000 and the stainless-steel-bracelet version is ¥968,000.

Japan-only run: 100 pieces

Case: 41 mm × 11.65 mm

Power reserve: approximately 42 hours

Water resistance: 3 bar

It costs more than ¥900,000

The black alligator-leather version is priced at ¥924,000, while the seven-row stainless-steel-bracelet version is ¥968,000. It is certainly not an impulse purchase.

For someone who follows Samurai Japan and wants a mechanical watch that can be worn beyond commemorative occasions, however, it is an intriguing proposition.

A subtle expression of Japan in indigo and crimson

This is a Samurai Japan collaboration, but viewed from the front it does not look like an ostentatious commemorative piece. The indigo dial reflects a traditional colour of Japan’s national baseball team, while the crimson GMT hand evokes the red of the national flag.

The caseback carries the Samurai Japan emblem and an “ONE OF 100” engraving, and the special brown leather-look box also uses the team logo. The limited-edition details are concentrated on the back and in the presentation box rather than dominating the dial. That restraint appeals to me.

A GMT function for travelling abroad

The crimson GMT hand is not just decoration. Used with the 24-hour scale, it lets you check two time zones, such as Japan and your destination abroad. That could be useful during overseas travel, business trips or when following a match abroad.

The dial remains relatively uncluttered while retaining the Navitimer’s familiar rotating slide rule.

A 41 mm case

The case measures 41 mm in diameter and 11.65 mm thick. Its automatic Breitling Calibre 32 has approximately 42 hours of power reserve.

Water resistance is 3 bar, so I would regard it as a mechanical watch for daily life and travel rather than a water-focused sports watch. With the leather strap, summer perspiration is another practical consideration.

Leather strap or bracelet

The leather strap offers a more understated feel. The seven-row bracelet is also appealing for summer and everyday use. The price difference is ¥44,000.

The official page describes the model as limited to 100 pieces in Japan, but it does not clearly state whether that figure covers both configurations together or each configuration separately. I would confirm that point with the retailer before buying.

What to check before buying

A limited edition is not guaranteed to rise in value. This is a watch to choose because you genuinely like the combination of indigo, crimson and Samurai Japan.

For a pre-owned example, I would check not only the watch but also the special box, warranty card, manuals, spare bracelet links and service history. Stock and delivery timing should be confirmed immediately before purchase.

Robu’s Take

This Navitimer is not a commemorative watch that puts the Samurai Japan logo front and centre. It expresses Japan more subtly through its indigo dial and crimson GMT hand.

Personally, I would like to see the actual watch and observe how that indigo changes in different light rather than judging it only through a display case or screen.
