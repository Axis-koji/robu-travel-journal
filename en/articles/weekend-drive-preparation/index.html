A ten-minute checklist before your weekend drive

Hello, I’m Robu.

Spend ten minutes checking the car, route and what you need to take. This practical checklist is designed to help you set off without rushing.

Always follow your vehicle’s owner’s manual and maintenance documentation for specified values and inspection procedures. If you notice anything unusual, such as a strange sound, warning light or leak, do not set off until the problem has been checked. Consult a garage or roadside assistance service.

Minutes 1–3: walk around the car

  • Tyres: Check pressure, cracks, damage, unusual wear and tread condition. Use the specified pressures shown near the driver’s door or elsewhere on the vehicle.
  • Under the car: Look for signs of fluid leaks.
  • Lights: Check headlights, brake lights and indicators, and look for dirty or damaged lenses.
  • Windows and mirrors: Check for dirt or mist that obstructs your view, and make sure the mirrors are adjusted correctly.

Minutes 4–6: the driver’s seat and engine area

  • Fuel or battery charge: Allow for the return journey and traffic delays, not only the trip to your destination.
  • Warning lights: Check that lights which should go out after starting the engine do so.
  • Brakes: Check whether pedal travel and feel differ from normal.
  • Wipers and washer: Check operation and fluid level.
  • Fluid levels: Check coolant, engine oil, brake fluid and other relevant fluids according to the vehicle manual.

Minutes 7–8: route and rest stops

Check the destination, journey time, traffic, roadworks and weather. Decide where you can stop for a break. Set the navigation before driving away, and share the estimated arrival time and possible rest stops with your passengers.

Minutes 9–10: insurance documents and supplies

  • Required documents, including your driving licence, vehicle inspection certificate and compulsory motor insurance certificate, as applicable in Japan
  • Smartphone, charging cable and power bank
  • Drinks, regular medication, glasses or sunglasses, rainwear and other essentials
  • The location of onboard equipment such as a warning triangle or emergency stop warning light
  • Contact details for roadside assistance and your insurer

Deciding not to set off is part of the check

Change your plans if you are very sleepy, unwell, facing weather that seriously limits visibility, or concerned about a vehicle fault. If an issue cannot be resolved within ten minutes, do not rush to leave: ask a professional.
