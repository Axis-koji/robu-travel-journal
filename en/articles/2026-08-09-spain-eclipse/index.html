A celestial show before sunset: choosing a clear western sky for Spain’s total eclipse

A brief period of totality just before sunset: this article brings together safe viewing advice and a sample itinerary based on local information.

This is the English edition of a research article based on official information available on August 9, 2026, Japan time. The travel section is a hypothetical itinerary, not the author’s personal experience. The schedules below reflect the article’s original date, not a new status check.

The original article looked ahead to the total solar eclipse on August 12, 2026, crossing Greenland, Iceland, Spain and other areas. In Spain, totality would occur as the Sun approached the western horizon, making a clear view to the west especially important when choosing a site.

Japan was not included in the viewing regions shown by NASA, but coverage was scheduled on NASA Live. Converting NASA’s announced US Eastern time to Japan time gave an expected broadcast start of around 2:15 a.m. on August 13.

Following a brief totality just before sunset

According to NASA, totality would last less than two minutes at many locations. In León in northern Spain, it was expected to begin at around 8:28 p.m. local time. With sunset approaching, viewers needed to choose a location where mountains, buildings and trees would not block the western sky.

Travellers heading to a viewing site from an airport or station were advised to avoid major journeys on the day and check public transport and traffic restrictions. Crowds and vehicles could concentrate immediately afterwards, so the return journey to accommodation also needed extra time.

Essential viewing precautions

  • Do not use ordinary sunglasses for eclipse viewing.
  • Use eclipse-viewing glasses compliant with ISO 12312-2 during the partial phases and before and after totality.
  • Do not look through a camera, binoculars or telescope while wearing eclipse glasses.
  • Optical equipment requires a dedicated solar filter fitted over the front, objective-lens end.
  • With pinhole projection, look at the projected image, not through the hole.

A sample itinerary, imagined on the ground

Arrive in a northern Spanish town in the early afternoon, leave your luggage at your accommodation, then head to the viewing location before the evening crowds. Check for mountains or tall buildings to the west and prepare eclipse glasses, drinking water, warm clothing and information for the journey back.

As the Sun gets lower, the city’s light gradually softens. Totality is brief, so a plan focused on safely watching the changing sky, with solar filters used correctly, may be more rewarding than spending the moment adjusting equipment.
