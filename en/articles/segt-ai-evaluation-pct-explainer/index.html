Can You Trust an AI Score? A Simple Guide to SEGT

Hello, this is Robu.

“What score would you give this essay?”

AI can answer quickly. But should we accept its score without asking how it reached that judgment?

SEGT is a technology intended to organize assessments using criteria set in advance. A useful starting point is to think of it as an attempt to improve the way scoring is organized—not a machine that always produces the right answer.

The same essay: 70 points or 90?

Imagine two people reading the same essay. One values clean writing and few spelling mistakes. The other wants more original ideas. Both take the task seriously, yet they may give different scores.

AI assessments can also change depending on what the evaluator is asked to prioritize. The scores here are an illustration, not results from a SEGT experiment.

What does SEGT do?

The developer describes a process combining human assessments, scoring criteria and generative AI. SEGT is the technology's name; eval000 is the evaluation system using it. Developer's explanation

The starting question is simple: What are we scoring?

For an essay, the criteria might cover clear ideas, supporting reasons and readability. A list of scoring items and their weights is called a “rubric”—in everyday language, a scoring guide.

SEGT aims to use these criteria and multiple assessments in repeated calculations to produce a reference evaluation value. That is different from describing it as simply averaging scores.

Does a consistent score mean a fair score?

Getting the same answer repeatedly does not prove that the answer is right.

Suppose a scoring guide rewards difficult vocabulary above everything else. Someone writing clearly in everyday language might lose points unfairly. A repeatable result would not fix that unsuitable scoring guide.

The developer's explanation of convergence also has conditions: the same evaluator group, inputs and calculation method. It does not establish fairness for every person or task.

Is it ready to make important decisions?

The developer presents demonstrations, but these alone do not establish that the system should decide school grades or hiring outcomes.

Before using it, three practical questions matter:

  • Who wrote the scoring guide, and what does it reward?
  • Can a person examine a result that seems wrong?
  • Can the person being assessed ask for a review?

Real-world results and independent testing are important when judging whether the approach is useful.

What is the international patent application about?

The news concerns a crowdfunding project seeking money for a PCT international application related to SEGT. The developer wants to pursue protection for the technology beyond Japan. Project explanation

That is a step toward seeking patent protection internationally, not an announcement that patents have been granted worldwide. Patent procedures and evidence that scoring is fair are separate matters.

One thing to remember

You do not need to memorize the name or the mathematics.

When AI gives a score, ask: “What criteria produced that score?”

That question helps when reviewing essays, business ideas or your own writing. Rather than stopping at “82 points,” ask what works and what needs improvement.

SEGT is interesting because it draws attention to how assessments are designed, rather than simply handing judgment to AI. Its practical value still needs to be assessed through further evidence.
