Should You Buy the Notta Memo Pro? Seven Checks Before Spending ¥29,700

Notta opened reservations for its new card-style AI voice recorder, the Notta Memo Pro, on September 28, 2026. The official release is scheduled for October 13 at a listed Japanese price of ¥29,700 including tax.

Its headline features are automatic recognition of phone versus in-person recording and automatic upload when the device returns to a trusted Wi-Fi network. That sounds useful for people who routinely forget to transfer recordings or struggle to find one important sentence later.

But buying the hardware does not provide unlimited transcription and AI summaries. Eligible recordings may move into a cloud workspace, and workplace conversations require consent and policy decisions. Here are seven points to check first.

1. Price and release timing

Notta lists September 28, 2026 as the reservation date, October 13 as the general release date, and ¥29,700 including tax as the price. Reservations are through Notta's official Japanese store; Amazon and Rakuten are also listed for the general release.

This article reflects information available on September 29. Promotions, inventory, shipping and return terms may vary by seller, so check the final order page.

2. Hardware: thin body, 64GB and a colour screen

Published specifications include a 77.2 × 56 × 3.9mm body, a 0.95-inch AMOLED colour display, 64GB of internal storage, USB-C, Web USB, Bluetooth 5.4 and Wi-Fi.

The original Memo had 32GB, a monochrome display and a proprietary magnetic cable. Doubling storage and moving to USB-C are straightforward improvements. However, the launch announcement does not list weight, continuous recording time or charge time. Buyers who prioritise portability or all-day recording should wait for a complete product page or manual.

3. Recording: automatic phone/in-person detection

The original model required manual switching between phone and in-person modes. Notta says Memo Pro recognises the scenario automatically.

That could remove a common source of user error, but the announcement does not prove identical performance across every handset, case or calling app. Treat this as a manufacturer-described feature, not a guarantee for every setup, and check compatibility information and independent hands-on testing after release.

4. Sync: useful, but subject to conditions

Automatic Cloud Sync starts when Memo Pro is idle, a recording is waiting, the device is within range of a trusted network, and battery is above 20 percent or the unit is connected to power. Notta's help page says the feature supports 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, not 5GHz-only networks, and remembers up to three trusted networks.

After a successful upload, the recording appears in the selected Notta workspace and is removed from Memo Pro. Recording takes priority and pauses sync. That workflow is convenient, but users should understand where the authoritative copy will live.

5. Transcription allowance: 600 minutes is not unlimited

Eligible users who bind a Memo Pro can receive the Notta Hardware Plan. Notta currently lists 600 transcription minutes per month, 100 AI summaries per month, 100 file imports per month and up to five hours per recording.

Binding several devices to the same workspace does not multiply the 600-minute allowance, and eligibility depends on workspace status. Heavy users and teams should include a Premium, Business or other paid plan in the real cost. Recheck the live pricing page immediately before subscribing.

6. Marking important moments

A short press of the REC button during recording adds a mark. After sync, it appears as a bookmark that can jump to the matching transcript or audio. A long press ends recording, so the distinction matters.

Bookmarks can reduce the need to replay an entire meeting, but they do not decide why a statement matters or what action should follow. Human review still completes the workflow.

7. Privacy and consent

Notta's security page states that data is encrypted in transit with TLS 1.2 and at rest with AES-256, that infrastructure is hosted on AWS, and that the company holds or follows several security and privacy frameworks including SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001. These are useful signals, but they do not automatically satisfy every employer policy, industry rule or client contract.

Memo Pro's automatic sync is a cloud workflow. Notta Desktop separately offers a local Privacy Mode, but that is a desktop feature; Memo Pro recordings should not be assumed to receive the same local-only treatment.

Before recording meetings, sales calls, medical consultations or other sensitive conversations, define notice and consent, storage location, access rights and deletion periods. Check workplace and client policies as well as applicable law.

Who is it for?

Memo Pro looks most suitable for people who record recurring meetings, want less manual transfer work, can operate a trusted 2.4GHz network and Notta workspace, and either fit within 600 minutes per month or have budget for a paid plan.

Consider waiting if weight and battery endurance are decisive, if recordings cannot leave local storage, if only 5GHz Wi-Fi is available, or if you expected unlimited transcription to be included in the hardware price.

Robu's verdict

The clearest value proposition is not simply recording quality. It is the reduction of post-recording work through automatic mode detection, trusted-Wi-Fi sync and one-button bookmarks.

Judge the ¥29,700 price together with transcription allowances, possible subscription costs, cloud governance and consent procedures. Buyers who need missing battery and weight details have a good reason to wait; teams that want recorded conversations to become searchable actions quickly have a credible reason to investigate further.
