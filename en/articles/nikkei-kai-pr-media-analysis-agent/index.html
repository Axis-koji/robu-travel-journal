What Is NIKKEI KAI's PR & Media Analysis Agent? Features and Checks Before Use

PR and investor-relations teams need to understand how their organization and its competitors are covered in the media. In practice, relevant articles are spread across multiple outlets, and comparing them or identifying trends takes time. Using generative AI also requires careful handling of copyright and each information provider's terms of use.

On September 15, 2026, Nikkei Inc. launched the PR & Media Analysis Agent within its enterprise generative AI service, NIKKEI KAI. Based on the official information, this article explains what the feature does, who may benefit from it, and what organizations should check before introducing the service.

Three ways it visualizes media coverage

The PR & Media Analysis Agent analyzes coverage in newspapers, magazines, trade publications, and other news media for a specified company or industry. The official announcement highlights three main features.

1. Charts for volume, themes, and outlets

The service charts changes in coverage volume, the share of coverage by theme, and trends by media outlet. These views can help users compare how their organization and its competitors are covered and identify which outlets account for more exposure.

2. Summaries and possible topics for future communications

It summarizes and analyzes coverage of the organization and its competitors, organizing recurring themes and tone. Based on existing coverage, it presents themes and issues that teams may consider in future communications.

3. Access to the supporting articles

Articles and charts include source references, allowing users to trace an analysis back to the underlying coverage. For business use, the ability to review the source material is important because users should not rely on an AI-generated explanation alone.

Two intended use cases

The official announcement identifies tracking an organization's share of overall media exposure—its share of voice—and pre-release research as two primary examples.

  • Monitor coverage volume and themes over time to understand the organization's position within its industry
  • Before drafting a press release, review competitors' coverage volume and themes to identify angles that could strengthen the organization's own communications

The service may be relevant to PR, IR, and marketing teams that currently spend substantial time researching and comparing coverage across multiple publications.

Organizations likely to benefit—and those that should assess the fit first

The feature may suit organizations that regularly track media trends for both themselves and their competitors, particularly teams that spend significant time collecting, organizing, and comparing articles.

Organizations with little media coverage, teams that monitor only one or two outlets, or companies mainly interested in social-media and word-of-mouth analysis should confirm whether the service covers the analysis they need and whether the cost is justified. The official announcement describes the analysis target as news media such as newspapers, magazines, and trade publications.

What to check before introducing the service

  • What are the pricing and contract terms? As of October 25, 2026, they are not shown on the public product pages and require an inquiry
  • Whether the media outlets important to the organization are included
  • Whether the analysis period and update frequency fit the team's workflow
  • What access rights or subscription conditions apply to source articles
  • Who will review AI-generated findings and make the final communication decisions

Pricing, contract details, implementation outcomes, and customer usage figures that are not stated in the official sources should not be treated as established facts. Before making a decision, an organization should compare its current research workload with the service's confirmed scope and ask Nikkei for the relevant commercial details.

Robu's Take

The PR & Media Analysis Agent is an enterprise feature designed to support the collection, comparison, and organization of media coverage. In addition to charts and summaries, it provides links to the source articles behind the analysis.

The generated analysis and suggested topics do not guarantee communications results. Organizations should confirm the covered publications, commercial terms, and internal review process, then compare the service with the time currently required for media analysis.
