The all-new Pajero brings genuine seven-seat cross-country strength and everyday comfort together in one compelling flagship. It layers modern driver assistance and a refined cabin onto the commanding character that made earlier Pajeros memorable.

Using Mitsubishi Motors' official releases and Japanese product pages, this guide compares the four grades and shows where their appeal fits into everyday life.

The Pajero name dates to 1982. Mitsubishi says the first four generations exceeded 3.29 million units of cumulative production and reached more than 170 countries and regions. The new model carries familiar cues, including a horizontal stance and a C-pillar inspired by the first generation's roll bar.

1. Price and grades: from ¥6,193,000 to ¥7,950,800

The four grades differ in more than price. Wheels, lighting, grille treatment and cabin equipment make each one easier to distinguish. Japanese suggested retail prices include consumption tax.

Mitsubishi lists 11 two-tone and monotone body colours, with availability varying by grade. The official page lets readers switch grades and colours while viewing the actual exterior in 360 degrees. Recycling fees, insurance, taxes other than consumption tax, registration and related charges are extra. The ¥572,000 gap between GLS and GSR adds equipment including leather upholstery, power seats, seat heaters and a rear differential lock.

2. Release and delivery: December 17 is not every grade's production date

Japanese sales begin through Mitsubishi dealers on December 17, 2026. Reservations opened on October 1, and Mitsubishi reported about 5,500 orders by October 28—more than its annual target, although the target volume was not stated in the sources reviewed here.

The product page says EXCEED and GLS are scheduled to enter production in April 2027. Production is in Thailand, with the grades rolling out in stages after launch.

3. Size and manoeuvrability: plan around the 1,925 mm width

The body measures 4,920 mm long, 1,925 mm wide and 1,910 mm high; GSR and GLS are 1,900 mm high. The wheelbase is 2,870 mm. Mitsubishi describes the dimensions as manageable from city streets to the outdoors, but the real test is the owner's parking environment.

Useful aids include a high seating position, a four-camera 3D multi-around monitor, front underfloor view and eight front-and-rear parking sensors.

The commanding 1,925 mm-wide body creates the presence and seven-seat space expected of a Pajero, while the camera and sonar systems support everyday manoeuvring.

4. Powertrain and fuel economy: 2.4-litre diesel, 12.1 km/L WLTC

Every grade uses four-wheel drive, an eight-speed automatic and a 2.4-litre clean-diesel turbo. The October 29 release lists 150 kW at 3,500 rpm and 480 N·m from 1,750 to 2,500 rpm.

WLTC fuel economy is 12.1 km/L for all four grades. That is a regulated test value; real consumption varies with conditions and driving. At the published figure, 10,000 km per year would require roughly 826 litres. Multiplying that by a local diesel price gives a planning estimate, not a promise of real-world cost.

5. Four-wheel drive, seven modes and a ladder frame

Super Select 4WD-II offers 2H, 4H, 4HLc and 4LLc. S-AWC integrates control of driving, turning and braking, while the seven drive modes are NORMAL, ECO, GRAVEL, SNOW, MUD, SAND and ROCK.

The ladder frame has been made stiffer while reducing weight. Suspension is double wishbone at the front and a newly developed five-link rigid axle at the rear. Mitsubishi says rough-road comfort was used as a development benchmark.

Ground clearance is 230 mm on models with 20-inch wheels. Mitsubishi also lists a 30.4-degree approach angle, 22.6-degree ramp breakover angle and 25.8-degree departure angle, all measured internally. The reviewed release does not give the corresponding figures for the 18-inch GSR and GLS.

6. Seven seats: test the third row with real passengers and luggage

The cabin measures 2,775 mm long, 1,575 mm wide and 1,235 mm high; SUPER EXCEED with a sunroof is 1,190 mm high. The second row slides through 150 mm and tumbles for third-row access. The third row is a 50:50 split bench that folds flat to expand the luggage area.

Mitsubishi says adults have headroom in the third row and can place their toes under the seat ahead. That is a manufacturer statement, not a substitute for sitting there. Families should test entry, posture and cargo space with the passenger arrangement they actually use.

7. MI-PILOT and e-Assist are driver assistance

The equipment table lists MI-PILOT as standard on every grade. It combines adaptive cruise control with full-speed-range following and lane-keeping assistance to reduce fatigue on expressways. Mitsubishi e-Assist includes collision-mitigation braking, misapplication collision prevention and Emergency Lane Assist, which the company says is a Mitsubishi first. Eight airbags include a centre airbag.

These are assistance systems, not autonomous driving. Mitsubishi explicitly warns that detection and control have limits and that the driver must not rely on the system. Grade differences also matter: adaptive LED headlights are listed for SUPER EXCEED and EXCEED, automatic high beams for GSR and GLS, and the digital rear-view mirror only for SUPER EXCEED.

Robu's Take

The all-new Pajero has the strength to carry seven family members or friends, head into snow or rough roads, and settle into a long-distance journey. A ladder frame, Super Select 4WD-II, seven drive modes and 480 N·m of diesel torque bring back the dependable, tool-like character that defines the name, now paired with contemporary comfort.

What stands out to me is how it retains the presence of earlier Pajeros while adding a genuinely useful seven-seat cabin and modern driver assistance. Its appeal lies in being a flagship made not only for imagining a weekend escape, but also for carrying a family's everyday life.
