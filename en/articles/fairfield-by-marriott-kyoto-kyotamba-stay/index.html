A Night at Fairfield by Marriott Kyoto Kyotamba: Local Food, Hot Springs and a Roadside-Station Breakfast

Hello, I’m Robu.

I stayed at Fairfield by Marriott Kyoto Kyotamba from September 26 to 27, 2026.

What made this stay memorable was not a long list of facilities inside the hotel. It was the way the hotel worked as a comfortable base for enjoying Kyotamba itself. The hotel stands next to the Kyotamba Ajimu-no-Sato roadside station, where travelers can eat local food and shop for seasonal produce and specialties.

After checking in, we drove to Springs Hiyoshi for a bath. We returned for black soybean udon and curry at the roadside station, bought local snacks for the room, and made breakfast from chestnut okowa (steamed glutinous rice with chestnuts), local eggs and grapes the following morning.

The short version: this hotel is best used as a base for exploring Kyotamba

Fairfield by Marriott Kyoto Kyotamba felt less like a self-contained resort and more like a clean, comfortable starting point for discovering the area.

Marriott’s official page highlights its location beside Kyotamba Ajimu-no-Sato. The rooms include a small refrigerator and a movable table, while complimentary coffee and tea are available in the lobby. Those simple features worked well for the way we traveled: buying local food nearby and enjoying it at the hotel.

A hot-spring visit after check-in

At check-in, we received a guest voucher offering a ¥200 discount on admission to Springs Hiyoshi. Offers can change, so guests should ask the hotel desk about the current benefit when they arrive.

According to the official Springs Hiyoshi information, the facility has a natural hot spring, heated pool, stone sauna and restaurant. The standard bathing-facility admission includes the hot spring and heated pool, while the stone sauna is charged separately. On this trip, however, we used only the hot spring; we did not use the pool or stone sauna.

We relaxed in the bath after the drive and then returned to the hotel. Because several ticket options are available, check the official information and ticket machine for the facilities you plan to use.

The guest rooms at Fairfield use a rain shower rather than a bathtub. For travelers who want a larger bath, combining the hotel with a nearby hot-spring visit makes good sense.

Black soybean udon and curry at Ajimu-no-Sato

Because we had eaten a late lunch before check-in, we wanted a simple dinner. At the Tamba cafeteria inside Kyotamba Ajimu-no-Sato, I chose plain black soybean udon, while my companion had curry rice. What looks like tempura in the photograph is actually complimentary tenkasu—crunchy tempura batter bits—that I added myself.

The roadside station’s official dining page says its black soybean udon is made at the facility’s own noodle workshop. It was an easy, casual way to try something closely connected to Kyotamba.

Dining hours and menus may change by season. If you plan to eat there in the evening, check the official website and last-order time before arrival.

Local snacks and café au lait in the room

After dinner, we bought yomogi mochi and black soybean bread at the roadside-station shop. The skewered snack visible on the left side of the photograph is not a Kyotamba product. It is “Hokkaido Specialty Toge no Ageimo,” which we had bought earlier at a Penguin Bakery in Kawanishi.

Penguin Bakery’s official product page describes it as a whole steamed potato coated in a lightly sweet batter and deep-fried. The version we bought was served on a skewer, as shown in the photograph.

Choosing what to bring back from the roadside station became part of the trip. Instead of having every meal arranged by the hotel, we could sample several local products at our own pace.

Two bottled drinks as a Platinum Elite welcome gift

I am a Marriott Bonvoy Platinum Elite member. At check-in, I was offered a choice between 500 welcome points and bottled drinks, and I chose the drinks.

The two bottles were lemonade and mikan cider. The labels identify Italian lemon juice in the lemonade and Japanese unshu mikan in the cider, but I could not verify that either product was made in Kyotamba. I therefore describe them only as the drinks offered during this stay, not as local Kyotamba products.

This describes the benefit offered during my stay. Elite benefits and available choices may change according to membership level, rate, property policy and date, so confirm the current options at check-in.

An outdoor breakfast made with food from the roadside station

The following morning, we brought breakfast to an outdoor table at the hotel. The seasonal highlight was chestnut okowa, a steamed glutinous-rice dish. The chestnuts were remarkably large, and the chewy rice made it especially satisfying. We also had boiled eggs made with local eggs, Fujiminori grapes, bread and a bottle of “Kyotamba Drinking Yogurt” from MILK FARM SUGIYAMA.

The Fujiminori grapes had fairly thick skins, but the juicy flesh slipped cleanly away from them. They were especially delicious and became another memorable part of the breakfast.

The drinking yogurt came in a 150 ml bottle. I could not finish it at breakfast and drank the rest in the car on the way home. It was not overly sweet, and its rich, thick yogurt flavor stood out.

The bottle is small in the breakfast photograph, so this separate image shows the package clearly. Its label identifies it as refrigerated fermented milk in a 150 ml bottle.

It was different from a restaurant breakfast, but selecting seasonal food and eating it outside made the morning feel closely connected to the place.

Marriott’s current official page says the hotel does not provide complimentary breakfast. Plans and optional meals may vary, so confirm the details when booking and consider the roadside station as another breakfast option.

Green views from the room

Our room had a simple, uncluttered design. Through the window, we could see the roadside station with green hills beyond it.

The landscape and fresh air stayed with me as much as any individual facility. A slow breakfast outside, with the surrounding greenery in view, was enough to make the stay feel distinctly Kyotamba.

What worked well, and what to plan ahead

What I liked

  • The roadside station is next to the hotel, making local meals and shopping easy to include in the stay.
  • It was easy to sample Kyotamba foods such as black soybean udon, chestnut okowa and black soybean bread.
  • Complimentary coffee and tea in the lobby were convenient for relaxing in the room.
  • As a Platinum Elite member, I could choose two bottled drinks instead of 500 welcome points.
  • The room was clean and practical as a base for a road trip.
  • The green surroundings made an outdoor breakfast especially pleasant.

What to check before arrival

  • Restaurants and shops at the roadside station have set hours; check them if you will arrive late.
  • Rooms use rain showers rather than bathtubs, so plan a nearby hot-spring visit if you want a larger bath.
  • Hotel-guest discounts and other local offers may change; confirm them at the front desk.
  • If you plan to buy breakfast nearby, check both opening hours and seasonal availability.

Robu’s Take

The appeal of Fairfield by Marriott Kyoto Kyotamba became clear when I stopped asking only what the hotel contained and started looking at where the hotel could take us.

For another way to explore the same area, see my Japanese article, A drive to Shizushi Limestone Cave, with an egg-and-rice lunch at Kominka Kitamura. Combining that day trip with a night at this hotel would make a relaxed Kyotamba itinerary.

We visited a hot spring, ate black soybean udon, brought local sweets and bread back to the room, and made our own breakfast from seasonal food the next morning. The stay felt like a collection of small discoveries from Kyotamba.

I would recommend it to travelers who enjoy driving through a region and choosing their own local food, rather than to someone looking for a full-service resort that keeps everything inside one building.
