Anker right-angle USB-C cable review for reaching a distant power source

Hello, I'm Robu.

Sometimes the problem with charging is not speed. It is a cable that falls just short, or a connector sticking out of the phone and getting in the way.

The Anker cable I use combines a length of about 1.8 metres with a right-angle connector at one end. Those two features make it useful when I want to bring power from somewhere a little farther away.

Reaching farther from a power bank

One device I use it with is the Anker Power Bank rated at 25,000mAh and 165W that I have previously reviewed. When that power bank's cable does not reach far enough, I connect this one instead.

I also use it to take power from the battery attached to my in-car refrigerator. The battery and the device I want to charge are not always close together. About 1.8 metres gives me useful room where a shorter cable would restrict the setup.

At home, the length is useful for reaching from a charging point to where I am using a device, including near the bed. With a longer cable, though, it is worth keeping it out of walkways, keeping charging devices uncovered and avoiding strain on the connectors.

For the power bank itself, see my Anker Power Bank 25,000mAh review in Japanese.

The right-angle end gets in the way less

A straight connector can protrude from my phone and catch against things or interfere with how I hold it. That was a recurring annoyance for me.

The right-angle end lets the cable run sideways from the phone. I find that it sticks out less awkwardly and reduces the annoyance of bumping the connector. It also fits better in places where a cable extending straight out would be inconvenient.

That is my experience of using it, not a claim that a right-angle connector cannot break. Check that its shape clears your phone case and any neighbouring ports.

Laptop charging speed depends on the connected equipment

I have also used this cable to charge my laptop. Being able to use it with both my phone and my computer is convenient.

Anker rates the cable for up to 240W. A cable alone does not make every device charge at that power: the charger or power bank and the device's supported input also determine the result. Anker's official FAQ explains that actual charging speed depends on the connected equipment.

It supports charging and data transfer, not video output, so it is not a monitor cable. Data transfer is rated at up to 480Mbps.

The Japanese product page also lists limitations for Samsung Super Fast Charging and fast charging on specified 16-inch MacBook Pro models. Do not assume the same fast-charging performance with every laptop; check the device-specific notes.

A reassuring connector and a reasonably priced two-pack

With an inexpensive cable I used previously, the connector came apart over time. Against that experience, this Anker cable feels solidly made, and I feel comfortable using it.

That does not mean all inexpensive cables are fragile, nor have I performed a durability comparison. It is simply the difference I have noticed with the cables I have used.

There are two cables in the box. I do not remember the exact amount I paid, but the set felt reasonably priced to me.

Anker Japan's official store listed the two-pack at ¥1,490 including tax (checked October 10, 2026).

Robu's Take

The maximum power rating is not the main reason I like this cable. The useful length and the right-angle end that gets in the way less are what make a difference in everyday use.

If you want to use a device farther from a power bank, or find straight connectors awkward, this is a cable worth considering. Choose it for how the length, connector direction and case clearance fit your own setup.
