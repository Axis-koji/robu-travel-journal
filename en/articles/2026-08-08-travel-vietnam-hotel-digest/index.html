Five things to check before a summer trip abroad: Vietnam and travel updates

Hello, I’m Robu.

This digest brings together official information about entry to Vietnam, prohibited items, personal security and planned hotels, alongside Middle East flight connections and travel advisories for Russia and Ukraine.

This is an English edition of editorial research checked against official information on August 8, 2026. It is not an account of the author’s reporting trip or hotel stay. Entry rules, flight operations and travel advisories change. The dated information below is not a fresh check: consult official sources again immediately before departure.

The short version

For the summer travel discussed in the original article, anyone considering Vietnam or a connection through the Middle East needed to check entry conditions, prohibited items, flights and Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs advisories—not just booking sites. Vietnam had no country risk advisory on the date checked, but importing or using e-cigarettes and heated-tobacco products, as well as theft and bag-snatching in cities, required specific attention.

Robu’s news digest

1. No country risk advisory does not mean no street-crime risk

When checked on August 8, 2026, the Ministry’s Vietnam page had no country risk advisory, spot advisory or infectious-disease risk advisory. However, its basic safety information reported pickpocketing, snatching and unattended-bag theft at tourist attractions, markets, on streets, in hotel lobbies and airports, and on trains and buses.

Avoid walking with your phone held out towards the road, carry bags in front of you, and keep watching your luggage during hotel check-in. These are three habits worth keeping from the start of a trip.

2. E-cigarettes and heated tobacco: not using them is not enough

The original article reports a comprehensive ban in Vietnam, from January 1, 2025, on producing, trading, importing, storing, transporting and using e-cigarettes and heated-tobacco products. According to an advisory from Japan’s Consulate-General in Da Nang, fines for use rose to VND3–5 million from May 15, 2026.

Transit is easy to overlook. Problems involving e-cigarettes in checked luggage were reported at Ho Chi Minh City’s airport even for transit passengers who did not enter Vietnam. Check before putting them in your luggage.

3. Up to 45 days visa-free for Japanese citizens, subject to conditions

The Ministry’s basic safety information described visa exemption for Japanese citizens holding valid Japanese passports for stays of up to 45 days. Conditions included at least six months’ passport validity on entry and a ticket to return home or continue to a third country.

Stays longer than 45 days, or trips outside the conditions, require an appropriate visa in advance. Vietnam’s Ministry of Public Security eVisa website described single- or multiple-entry electronic visas valid for up to 90 days. Check the address carefully because unofficial sites can imitate the application service. These conditions are nationality-specific and dated; confirm those applying to your trip.

4. Growing visitor numbers: check availability and prices early

Statistics published by Vietnam’s government electronic newspaper put international visitors in the first half of 2026 at approximately 12.3 million, up 14.9% year on year. Around 10.1 million arrived by air, representing 82.6% of the total.

This does not mean places will necessarily be crowded. It suggests that demand for hotels in popular cities or on weekends, domestic flights and airport transfers may be stronger. That is an editorial inference from the statistics, so check actual prices and availability individually before booking.

5. New hotel plans around Ho Chi Minh City are appealing—but still in the future

IHG announced plans for four hotels with more than 1,000 rooms in Can Gio, Ho Chi Minh City. Holiday Inn Express and Garner were planned for 2028, with InterContinental and Crowne Plaza in 2030. A separate announcement placed Crowne Plaza Saigon Binh Duong in 2029 and Regent Ho Tram in 2030.

These are future developments, not hotels to describe as currently bookable. Include them in a real itinerary only after the brand’s official booking page confirms opening and sales availability.

A useful order for travel preparations

  1. Check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs’ safety information for both your destination and transit points.
  2. Check airline websites for cancellations and route changes.
  3. Confirm entry rules, passport validity and visa requirements.
  4. Check rules for bringing in items such as e-cigarettes, medicines and cash.
  5. Then confirm that your hotel is open, its booking conditions and transfer arrangements.

Hotel announcements inspire travel ideas, but announced, under construction, accepting bookings and open are four different stages. Do not confuse them when reading articles or making a reservation.

Regional notes for travellers

Connections through the Middle East

The Ministry’s regional advisory dated July 21, 2026 warned that a sudden deterioration in the Middle East could lead to flight cancellations or temporary airspace closures. As of July 23, JAL’s notice listed Haneda–Doha flight JL059 as cancelled through October 23 and JL050 through October 24. Even with a ticket issued, check the airline’s current operations immediately before departure.

Russia and Ukraine

The original article described all of Ukraine as Level 4, an evacuation recommendation with advice not to travel. Russia’s areas near the Ukrainian border were Level 4, with the rest generally Level 3. Restrictions also affected direct flights, international transfers and international payment cards. Do not casually treat these as ordinary holiday destinations; check the Ministry’s country pages for current advice.

A five-minute pre-departure check

  • Check the remaining validity of your passport.
  • Check your return or onward ticket.
  • Make sure e-cigarettes and heated-tobacco products are not packed.
  • Check flights and risk advisories, including transit points.
  • Register with Japan’s Tabi-Regi travel notification service where applicable.
  • Confirm the hotel is open through its official booking page.
