Is the Canon EOS R8 Mark II Worth Buying? Checking 546 g, 5-Axis Stabilization, Autofocus and 4K/60p in 7 Points

Full-frame is appealing, but if it is big and heavy, it ends up staying at home. The Canon EOS R8 Mark II, released on October 29, 2026, looks like a very encouraging answer to that problem. A body of about 546 g with battery and card (lens excluded) holds 5-axis in-body image stabilization, an effective 24.2-megapixel full-frame CMOS sensor, upgraded autofocus and high-definition 4K/60p video built on 6K oversampling.

I have not used the camera myself. Based on Canon's official information, this article looks at how these features might translate into value for family, kids, pets, travel, street walks, night scenes and video work, in seven points. At a price of roughly ¥200,000 to ¥300,000, I wanted it to be clear what you are paying for.

1. A compact 546 g body with 5-axis in-body image stabilization

The first appeal is that portability and ease of shooting live in the same body. It weighs about 546 g with battery and card, excluding the lens, which is light enough to make you want to put it in your everyday bag.

On top of that, it has 5-axis in-body image stabilization. The correction is optimized for RF and EF lenses, and it works in coordination with compatible RF lenses. For stills, under the specific conditions Canon states, the stabilization effect is up to 7.5 stops at the center and 7.0 stops at the periphery.

Evening on a trip, a dim shrine precinct, a celebration indoors: daily life is full of places where setting up a tripod is awkward. In those moments, a light body and a strong stabilization mechanism support the freedom to shoot by hand. For video, it is also reassuring when walking and shooting through town.

2. A 24.2-megapixel full-frame sensor, DIGIC X and ISO 100–102400 widen your expression

The foundation of image quality is the effective 24.2-megapixel full-frame CMOS sensor and the DIGIC X processor. The standard ISO range is 100 to 102400, so you can choose a sensitivity to suit everything from bright outdoors to dim interiors.

About 24.2 megapixels is a comfortable resolution for sharing on social media, family albums and even prints. The generous rendering of full-frame adds to the pleasure of portraits with softly blurred backgrounds and wide travel landscapes.

A wide ISO range directly expands your freedom. Evening streets, an illuminated garden, a softly lit restaurant: many memorable scenes happen when light is scarce, so having room in sensitivity is welcome.

3. Registered-person priority and subject detection make it easier to follow family, pets and vehicles

Autofocus is one of the biggest advances. The camera carries Dual Pixel CMOS AF II, with improved tracking of the main subject compared with the EOS R8.

Registered-person priority is the highlight. It works for both stills and video. Because the AF prioritizes a person you have registered in advance, it is reassuring when you want your child in a crowd or a loved one in a group shot. For people it detects eyes, faces, heads and torsos, which helps keep the main subject in view as direction and distance change.

Detection does not stop at people. For animals it supports dogs, cats, birds and horses, and for vehicles it supports motorsports, trains and aircraft. A dog on a walk, a wild bird in the garden, a train pulling into the station, a plane overhead at the airport. You can follow subjects across genres, which suits anyone who wants to widen their hobbies.

4. About 40 fps with the electronic shutter for moments full of motion

With the electronic shutter, you can shoot continuously at up to about 40 frames per second. Forty frames in a single second is a strong ally when you do not want to miss a fleeting expression or movement.

A child breaking into a run, a dog leaping for a ball, a bird taking off, a train rounding a curve. In scenes like these, combining burst speed with the autofocus above makes it easier to aim at "that moment." Choosing the best expression from a burst is also part of the fun of shooting.

It looks set to be a big help for sports days, playground afternoons and playtime with pets. The light body also means less strain from holding the camera up, which counts when you are following movement.

5. 4K/60p built on 6K oversampling, and freedom in video making

For video, the highlight is high-definition 4K/60p based on 6K oversampling. Generating 4K from 6K-class information promises detailed footage, and 60p keeps movement smooth, so travel scenery, a child's activity or a pet's movements stay lively.

For people who enjoy making videos, support for Canon Log 3 and Look File/LUT is welcome. Building color after shooting is a pleasure, and you can grow a look of your own. Movie electronic IS offers an "Enhanced+" setting, handy when you want to prioritize stabilization while walking and shooting.

There are also Skin Smoothing Movie, Product Review Movie and Stabilized Movie modes, along with 180p Full HD and slow and fast motion. You can slow down a moment you want to savor or speed up a scene to keep the tempo. From product reviews and vlogs to family records, it is flexible enough to cover them all with one camera.

6. Night scene colors, filters, handling and connectivity that make shooting fun

The EOS R8 Mark II also has features that make the act of shooting itself enjoyable.

One is the color setting for handheld night scene mode. Five colors are available, Standard, Blue, Black&Orange, Pink and Green, so you can finish a night city mood to your own taste. Fourteen of the still-photo color filters are also offered as free video LUTs, letting you play with color in both photos and movies.

For handling, it has an 8-direction multi-controller. The front tally lamp shows the video recording state, so people in front of the camera can tell whether you are recording. The multi-accessory shoe lets you add accessories to widen your shooting style.

For connectivity, it supports 5 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Sending photos to a smartphone to share is smooth, which can add to the pleasure of showing each other the day's photos while you are still on the trip.
