Should You Buy Crazy Taxi: World Tour? Seven Confirmed Details Before Release

Pick up a passenger, beat the clock, and earn more by driving with style. The arcade series that began in 1999 is returning as a full new entry, Crazy Taxi: World Tour.

SEGA currently plans to release the game in 2027. A precise date and price have not been announced. Even so, SEGA's Tokyo Game Show 2026 announcements and closed network test information provide a much clearer picture of the game. Here are seven details confirmed by official sources—and the gaps that should keep buyers from deciding too early.

1. Five current platform families are announced

SEGA lists the following platforms:

  • Xbox Series X|S
  • Xbox on PC
  • PC via Steam
  • PlayStation 5
  • Nintendo Switch 2

The release window is 2027, while price and CERO rating remain undecided. Steam also lists the final CPU and GPU requirements as TBD, so PC players should not make hardware purchases based on the store page yet.

2. The solo game expands into a globe-trotting story

Axel returns as the protagonist. The campaign follows his attempt to recover a stolen taxi from a mysterious group while traveling through five cities. Official descriptions mention missions, side jobs, varied terrain, unlockable vehicles, and customization.

SEGA also unveiled a Japan-inspired map in September 2026. The direction appears to preserve the series’ immediate arcade energy while giving solo players a stronger long-form objective.

3. The classic time-pressure loop remains in Arcade Mode

SEGA’s Steam description says Arcade Mode will revisit the original race-against-the-clock structure, with players trying to earn as much as possible before time expires.

That matters because World Tour is not replacing its score-attack roots with a story campaign. It is presenting both: a repeatable short-session mode for returning fans and a broader campaign for newcomers.

4. Online play has been tested with up to six players

SEGA’s TGS 2026 page describes cooperative and competitive online play for up to six players. The September closed network test included two modes:

  • Pickup Race, a competition to deliver passengers to their destinations first; and
  • Cops 'n' Cabbies, a team battle between taxis and police.

The test also offered ranked and custom matches, and cross-play could be disabled.

Those details are not a guarantee of the final configuration. The test covered only part of the multiplayer game, and SEGA’s product summary still lists the final online player count as undecided. Modes, limits, and matchmaking rules may change before launch.

5. The network-test PC requirements are not final requirements

The closed test recommended Windows 11, 16GB of RAM, an RTX 4060 8GB or Radeon RX 6700 XT 12GB, an SSD, and 12GB of storage for a stated 1080p, high-quality, 60fps target.

The same page explicitly warns that the retail version’s minimum and recommended specifications may differ. These numbers describe a development build, not a safe final buying guide.

6. Generative AI was used for ideation, not final game assets

SEGA’s developer disclosure on Steam says generative AI was used as an optional support tool when brainstorming background objects. It also states that no AI-generated content will be included in the final game.

That distinction—using a tool during ideation versus shipping its generated output—is useful information for players who consider creative process and rights practices when buying games.

7. A wishlist is available; a preorder is not

The official site and platform stores accept wishlist registrations. Price, release date, preorder bonuses, and editions have not been announced. A wishlist is simply a way to receive updates; it is not a purchase commitment.

Verdict: watch closely if you want both the arcade core and a modern structure

The current appeal is easy to identify:

  • the short-session score attack survives;
  • a five-city solo campaign adds structure;
  • up-to-six-player online modes have been tested; and
  • the game is planned for five current platforms.

It is still too early for a firm buying recommendation. Price, exact date, final PC requirements, and the final online player count remain open. If the combination of classic handling, a new campaign, and multiplayer sounds right, adding the game to a wishlist is reasonable. A final decision can wait for platform comparisons, performance targets, cross-play details, monetization information, and the complete soundtrack policy.
