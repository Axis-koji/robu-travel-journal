Casio OCEANUS OCW-P3000: A World-Time CACHALOT Built for Global Travel

Casio's OCEANUS CACHALOT OCW-P3000 approaches world time as information you can scan, not a menu you have to navigate. Its city-code ring and rotating 24-hour bezel work together to show the approximate time—and whether it is day or night—across multiple regions.

This guide is based on Casio's official announcement and product pages. I have not worn or operated the watch, so the article does not make claims about comfort, long-term durability or hands-on usability.

Time Zone Navigator makes world time easier to scan

The defining feature is Casio's Time Zone Navigator. City codes sit around the outer edge of the dial. Holding the button at 10 o'clock moves the seconds hand to a city that is currently in the midnight hour. Aligning the bezel's triangle with that city lets the 24-hour scale indicate the approximate time in the other listed cities.

Casio lists 26 cities across 38 time zones, plus UTC, with daylight-saving settings and home-city swapping. A dual-dial display can show two cities at once. When paired with the CASIO WATCHES app, the world-time database expands to approximately 300 cities.

A two-layer display separates day from night

The rotating 24-hour bezel uses a two-tone treatment to distinguish daytime from 6:00 to 18:00 and nighttime from 18:00 to 6:00. Inside it sits the city-code ring. Together they form the visual interface for Time Zone Navigator rather than serving as decoration alone.

Titanium construction with a ceramic bezel insert

The case and bracelet are titanium, while the rotating bezel uses a scratch-resistant ceramic insert. The blue model receives a titanium-carbide treatment; the black model uses DLC coating. A sapphire crystal with anti-reflective coating on both sides covers the dial.

Official specifications for the OCW-P3000-2AJF list a 47.6 × 42.9 × 11.7 mm case, a weight of 99 g and water resistance to 10 bar. The solid-titanium bracelet fits wrists from 150 to 195 mm.

Bluetooth, radio control and Tough Solar

Time correction combines Multiband 6 radio reception in Japan, North America, Europe and China with Bluetooth synchronization through the CASIO WATCHES app. App features include automatic time correction, world-time settings, Time & Place, watch-status information and self-check functions.

The watch is powered by Tough Solar. Casio rates it for approximately five months of normal operation, or approximately 23 months in power-saving mode, after a full charge without further light exposure. It is also the first OCEANUS model to use TOUGH MVT. 2, which detects shocks and strong magnetic fields and controls the hands accordingly.

Robu’s Take

The OCW-P3000 stands out because its city-code ring and 24-hour bezel turn world time into a quick visual check. Titanium, ceramic, Bluetooth, radio control and solar charging also make sense for a travel-focused watch, particularly in a case that Casio lists at 11.7 mm thick. For people who coordinate across several regions every day, the function itself may be the strongest reason to consider it. Since I have not handled the watch, I would still recommend trying it on and testing the controls before buying.
