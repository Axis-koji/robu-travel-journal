A small backup for my everyday bag: Anker Nano Power Bank review

Hello, I'm Robu.

For travel, I want a large power bank. For an ordinary day out, I prefer something smaller. That is where my Anker Nano Power Bank fits in.

I use it less as a way to restore my phone to a full charge and more as a small reserve for when its battery runs out away from home. Here is what I like about using it.

A different job from a large power bank

My phone has a large battery, and this little power bank does not bring it all the way back to full in my use. Even so, being able to add charge when I run out away from home is genuinely useful.

The 5,000mAh figure describes the power bank's internal battery capacity; it does not mean all of that charge can be transferred to the phone. Voltage conversion and charging involve energy losses, and using the phone while charging consumes power too. Whether it reaches a full charge depends on the phone, its starting battery level and how it is used.

I use a larger Anker for travel and longer outings, and this smaller one for my everyday bag. Choosing according to the outing suits me better than trying to make one battery do everything.

A backup for the daily commute

I often take it on an ordinary trip to work and back, rather than on a holiday. Sometimes I leave in the morning having forgotten to charge my phone. On other outings, I use the phone more than expected and its remaining battery starts to worry me. Having this small power bank in my bag lets me add charge before the phone runs out.

I would rather avoid an unplanned battery purchase or a paid charging rental when possible. That is another reason I carry this one. Its weight and bulk do not bother me much, and it fits easily into my everyday bag.

Of course, the backup battery needs charging too. Simply carrying it is not enough; checking its indicator lights before heading out is a useful precaution.

The convenience of a built-in USB-C connector

The foldable USB-C connector plugs directly into a compatible phone, so I do not have to take out a separate charging cable. That simplicity is one of the things I like about it.

The side USB-C port can recharge the power bank, and it also allows cable charging of a phone. Direct connection is not the only option.

Charging has felt fairly quick in my use, too. In my setup, I have also been able to recharge the power bank from my computer's USB-C port. Having that option is useful if I forget my charger.

A USB-C socket alone does not guarantee this will work: the computer must support supplying power, and its settings can matter. Charging speed also depends on the source and cable. If the computer is running on battery, I am using some of its remaining charge to top up the power bank.

Case compatibility is worth checking first. Anker says cases with a bottom thickness over 5mm or a connector opening under 13mm, among others, are incompatible. See the official product page for the conditions.

The manual also instructs users to keep both the phone and the power bank on a stable surface when charging through the foldable connector. Avoid putting strain on that connection. Official manual

A simple indication of the remaining charge

The lights give me an approximate idea of the power bank's remaining battery. They do not display an exact percentage, but it is useful to be able to check a device I carry as a backup.

Specifications and price

Its full product name is Anker Nano Power Bank (22.5W, Built-In USB-C Connector). The main official specifications are:

  • Capacity: 5,000mAh
  • Weight: approximately 102g
  • Dimensions: approximately 77 × 37 × 25mm
  • Foldable USB-C connector and a USB-C port
  • Listed price in Japan: ¥3,990 including tax, checked October 10, 2026

These figures come from Anker Japan's official product page. The price is the listing observed on that date, not my purchase price, and may change. The 22.5W in the product name does not mean every phone receives that output; Anker lists compatibility conditions, so I would not choose it on charging speed alone.

I consider it an affordable backup. More important than price alone, though, is whether its role fits your needs: carry something small and add charge when necessary.

Robu's Take

It does not fully recharge my phone, but my purpose is to have a reserve for commuting and ordinary outings. With that role in mind, I like its small size and ease of use.

A large battery when I need plenty of charging, and a small backup for ordinary days: if that distinction matches your routine, this is an option worth considering.
