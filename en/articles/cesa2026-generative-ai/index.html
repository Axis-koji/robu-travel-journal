85.8% of game developers use generative AI at work: the role of human oversight in CESA’s official survey

Hello, I’m Robu.

New official figures offer a clearer picture of generative AI use in the games industry. Drawing on CESA’s 2026 survey, this article looks at what those figures mean and how human checks, corrections and oversight feature in day-to-day use.

CESA’s official announcement

On September 17, 2026, the Computer Entertainment Supplier’s Association (CESA) released the preview edition of the CESA Games Industry Report 2026 to coincide with Tokyo Game Show 2026. The report includes results from two original surveys: one of individual game developers and another of CESA member companies.

One particularly striking figure is that 85.8% of game developers use generative AI in their work. The announcement also identifies greater efficiency and productivity as the benefits CESA member companies most expect from using generative AI.

The full report is scheduled for release in early December. Detailed information about the number of respondents, the questions and the breakdown by job role has not yet been disclosed, so those details need to be checked in the full edition.

What “85.8%” does—and does not—mean

The figure refers to the proportion of game developers using generative AI somewhere in their work. It does not mean that AI creates 85.8% of game content. It may include use in individual tasks, such as drafting text, creating initial visual concepts or assisting with code completion.

The number shows how many developers are using generative AI, but it does not establish that generative AI has become the main driving force in game creation.

Human checks, corrections and oversight remain central

According to the official announcement, human checking, correction and supervision were the most commonly reported measures for managing generative AI use. Specifying or restricting the tools used, and not using generated output unchanged, were also common practices.

This suggests that the industry’s discussion is moving beyond whether to use the technology to how to use it with appropriate controls.

What this means for players

The emphasis on human checks, corrections and oversight also matters to players. Even when generative AI supports the work, the approach described places human review and judgment between that work and the finished release.

However, this is a trend reported by the survey. How extensively AI is used, and how that use is supervised, will vary from one game to another.

Robu’s Take

CESA’s official 2026 survey reports that 85.8% of game developers use generative AI at work. It also describes practices centred on human checks, corrections and oversight.

Rather than focusing only on the size of the number, I want to keep track of what it measures and how people remain involved, including the detail provided when the full report is released.
