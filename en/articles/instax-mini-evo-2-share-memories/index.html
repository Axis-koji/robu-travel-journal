instax mini Evo 2 Is a Camera That Makes You Want to Hand Someone a Print

instax is not only a camera for showing a photo—it lets you give one away

instax arrived in 1998. In April 2025, Fujifilm announced that cumulative sales had passed 100 million units. The brand is sold in more than 100 countries, and fiscal 2025 sales reached 200 billion yen, marking a fifth consecutive record year since fiscal 2021.

Its lasting appeal in the age of digital cameras and smartphones is captured by Fujifilm's own words: “Not just to take—but to give.”

Imagine the owner of a café you got talking to on a trip, a fellow traveler you met at the next table, a friend you have not seen in years, or a family member celebrating a birthday. Instead of ending with everyone looking at the same phone screen, you can turn the moment into a small photograph and say, “This is for you.”

The photograph stays in that person's hands. That is something simply showing an image on a smartphone cannot recreate.

The mini Evo 2 is a hybrid camera that lets you choose an image on-screen before printing it. You can keep the same photograph for yourself and transfer a printed image to the dedicated app afterward. The person who receives the print and the person who took it can each keep a memory of the same moment.

A few words in the border can turn a photograph into a tiny letter

Image note: This is an AI-generated editorial image expressing the pleasure of writing a date or message in the border of an instax print. It is not an official product photograph or official sample image.

instax prints have a white border around the image. Add a date, a place or a short “For you” with a permanent marker, and an ordinary photograph becomes a tiny letter written for one person.

Fujifilm's official media also introduces ways to decorate the border with permanent markers, messages, dates and illustrations.

The picture takes about 90 seconds to appear after printing. Even the time spent waiting together to see the finished image can become part of the memory.

Slip it into a notebook, place it on the refrigerator, or carry it in a wallet. If its colors change a little over the years, that too becomes evidence of the time that has passed since that day. Alongside memories revisited on a smartphone, you gain a memory you can physically hold.

A 4.5-inch touchscreen for shooting, choosing and finishing

The mini Evo 2 has a 4.5-inch rear display. It is about 1.5 times larger than the display on the previous mini Evo and is the first touchscreen in the instax series.

Official image: Quoted from Fujifilm's official “instax mini Evo 2 Product Design” page. The 4.5-inch LCD touchscreen occupies most of the rear, with the print lever visible on the right.

You can swipe through the pictures you have taken, choose the one you want to print, and adjust its crop, brightness, contrast and saturation on the camera.

After taking several photographs at a restaurant during a trip, everyone can choose the one to give away, make a quick adjustment and pull the print lever. The whole group can enjoy the sequence until the print emerges from the camera.

The new instax Expanded View displays an area slightly wider than the final print. It could make framing easier when following a running child or a pet that will not sit still.

Expanded View records at 6 megapixels when enabled, while turning it off and saving to a microSD card allows up to 13 megapixels. Rather than simply being a drawback, it lets you choose between easier composition and higher-resolution recording for the moment at hand.

Find your own look among 100 combinations

There are 10 lens effects and 10 film effects, creating 100 possible combinations.

For a café photograph with friends, you might combine soft color with soft focus. At the beach at sunset, a light-leak effect could let the glow spill across the image. Turn the lens and film dials, check the result on the large monitor and search for the mood you want to preserve.

The camera can also print photographs from a smartphone. You could print a landscape from an earlier trip, or a photograph from the last time you met someone, and give it to them when you meet again. Because it is both a camera and a printer, it opens up uses that either device alone cannot offer.

The five seconds before the shutter become a short memory

Another new feature is instax Immersive Movie. It records a five-second movie that includes the moment the shutter is released.

It can preserve the instant someone unexpectedly smiles while you frame the shot, or the way their hair moves in the wind. The motion just before the photograph was made can bring back the atmosphere surrounding that single frame.

The vertical movie can be played on the camera or transferred to the dedicated app. It has no sound, but like an old silent film, five seconds of movement alone can be enough to return a memory. A microSD card is required.

The printed photograph is something you can give away. The five seconds immediately before it remain with you as a small moving memory. The instax experience no longer ends with the paper print.

A tool whose design makes you want to carry it

The mini Evo 2 inherits a premium, classic appearance while introducing a curved body and a lens ring designed for a comfortable grip.

Turn the lens dial, choose a film effect and finally pull the print lever. The clicks and tactile response make operating the camera part of the pleasure in a way a smartphone cannot match.

It is a device for making photographs, a camera you want to carry on a trip, and an object whose controls are enjoyable in their own right.

Robu's Take

I think the mini Evo 2 takes the pleasure instax has always valued—handing someone a photograph on the spot—and makes it freer with today's technology.

Choose on the touchscreen, select a mood from 100 creative combinations, write a few words in the border and hand it over. A shared moment remains in the other person's hands. The five seconds just before the photograph remain with you as a movie.

It is for travelers who want to give a photograph to someone they meet, for families and friends who want a memory they can touch, and for people who enjoy shaping an image by turning dials and pulling a lever. For them, even waiting for launch can feel exciting.

The camera is scheduled to launch in Japan on November 27, 2026. I am looking forward to it too, imagining the moment when I capture a photograph I want to give to someone.
