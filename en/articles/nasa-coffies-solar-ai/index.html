Can we anticipate sunspots? Reading NASA’s AI research through a traveller’s eyes

Hello, I’m Robu.

Could we identify active regions on the Sun before they appear? It is a challenging topic for everyday news, so this article briefly considers its relationship to flight information and communications.

This is an editorial explanation based on NASA and publicly available information. Rather than reaching conclusions from social-media posts or fragments of news, it considers the possible effects by route, region and announcement date.

In simple terms

The research aims to use AI to recognise solar active regions before they emerge. It remains research, with results suggesting the possibility of an approximately 12-hour lead time; it should not be read as an established operational forecasting guarantee.

What the NASA research means

The value discussed in COFFIES is not just detecting regions associated with solar activity, but the possibility of seeing them earlier. At this stage, it is too early to use the findings directly for everyday operational decisions.

In other words, it is important to separate interpreting experimental results from making real-world operational decisions.

Notes for travellers

  • Major events are the main concern for substantial effects, and the impact differs by region and route.
  • Prioritise official operational information and check your airline’s guidance for the day.
  • Do not cancel a trip on the basis of a single news story. Consider both NOAA’s R/S/G impact scales and actual operational guidance.
  • Do not reach a conclusion from images shared on social media alone. Read the official primary information too.
