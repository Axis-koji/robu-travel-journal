Takara Tomy's familiar blue railway track now carries an airplane. The company released the Plarail Air “Flying Around the Sky! ANA Passenger Plane Set” in Japan on October 1, 2026. Its suggested retail price is ¥8,800, and the official age rating is three and older.[1][2]

The appeal is more than an airplane circling a level loop. Its wheels retract on takeoff, the body banks through curves and the wheels extend again for landing, all on a raised route.[1][2] This is not a hands-on review; it explains the mechanism and specifications using Takara Tomy's official release and product pages.

The short verdict

The set's main appeal is that it uses ascending and descending track and elevated curves to turn takeoff, banking and landing into a repeatable sequence. The box includes the plane, motor unit, specialized rails, terminal station, supports and a play mat, making an airport scene possible without first purchasing a separate core set.[1][2]

It is not a fully compatible expansion in which ordinary Plarail trains can use every new rail. Takara Tomy specifically says that standard Plarail vehicles cannot run on the ascent/descent rails, Air curves or Air stop rail.[2] Think of it as a new airplane branch that can share a toy city with existing trains and cars, not as a replacement for ordinary railway track.

1. Price and core specifications

The suggested retail price is ¥8,800 including tax. The Japanese launch date was October 1, 2026, and the age rating is three and older. Takara Tomy lists the product size at approximately 410mm wide, 115mm deep and 310mm high. The motor requires two AA alkaline batteries, sold separately.[1]

The age rating does not mean a child can assemble the entire layout alone. For the first setup, an adult can check the instructions and help with supports and batteries. Takara Tomy provides an instruction-manual search portal.[4]

That product-size figure is not the same as the footprint of a completed layout. The official product page shows an expanded example measuring roughly 1,330mm by 820mm by 190mm.[2] A family does not have to build that exact arrangement, but the example makes the practical point: this is a toy that needs floor or table space as well as somewhere to store its parts.

2. How the plane appears to fly

The airplane is guided by its specialized track. Rather than just circling at one height, it climbs an ascent section, travels through elevated curves and follows a descent section. Those changes in height give the takeoff and landing moments a visible beginning and end.[1][2]

This is neither free flight nor a radio-controlled aircraft. A guided route makes the same sequence easy to repeat for a young child, but it does not provide open-ended steering. Buyers looking for remote piloting should consider a different toy category.

3. Landing gear and banking motion

Takara Tomy highlights retracting wheels during takeoff, extending them for landing and a banking motion through curves. In particular, the plane tilts into a turn instead of sliding around it level; that is one of the distinctive things to watch.[1][2]

The official images show the wheel retraction on takeoff and the aircraft banking in a turn. Both links open images on Takara Tomy's website.

Those movements help a child turn a loop into a story: boarding, takeoff, cruise and landing. The official specification, however, cannot tell us how loud the motor feels in a quiet room, how smooth the action remains at low battery or how well the mechanism tolerates years of play. Follow the manual and avoid forcing the aircraft or landing-gear parts by hand.

4. What is in the box

The official product page lists one ANA passenger-plane body, one motor unit, one Number 1 Terminal Station, one straight rail, eight Air curves, two ascent/descent rails, one Air stop rail, twelve rail holders, six double-height block supports, one map sheet and a sticker sheet.[2]

That is enough for the central airplane activity. The missing everyday item is power: the two AA alkaline batteries are not included.[1] Anyone giving the set as a present should add batteries so the first play session does not stop at unboxing.

5. Combining it with Plarail and Tomica

Takara Tomy says families can add separately sold Plarail trains and Tomica cars to the included map, creating rail access to the airport and road traffic around it.[1] The terminal station is presented as a combined rail station and airport terminal.[2]

The compatibility is thematic and spatial. It means the toys can form one city; it does not mean the train can travel over the specialized airplane curves. Adding existing trains and cars to the scene also increases the required floor area.

Robu's Take

Plarail Air is not simply another train on the same track standard. It is a new branch that converts a passenger plane's takeoff, bank and landing into guided play. The ¥8,800 set combines an elevated flight route with an airport scene.

The Japanese set is an ANA collaboration.[1] Takara Tomy separately plans to roll out an Asian-market edition with an original red aircraft design in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and other Asian markets.[1] The announcement does not say that the ANA edition itself will be sold overseas, so buyers using international listings should distinguish the red Asian edition from the Japanese ANA set.

The specialized rails, layout footprint and separate batteries are the important cautions. If possible, watch an official demonstration or see a store display before buying. That can answer the questions a press release cannot—especially sound, setup time and how the motion feels in person.
