A Warm Dish for a Tired Evening—Let the recolte RSY-2 Handle It

Hi, I'm Roboo.

After a long day, a warm dish can make the table feel a little more comforting. On some nights, that picture is enough to make me think, “Maybe not today.”

The RSY-2's country of production, however, is China. First, a note: I haven’t used this product myself. This article is based on the official product page, the official landing page, and the instruction manual, and it is my attempt to imagine what meals might look like with one at home.

A Kitchen Where You Can Do Something Else While You Wait

According to the official description, the RSY-2 automatically handles chopping the ingredients, adjusting the heating temperature, and stirring.

What appeals to me about this design is that there would be fewer reasons to stay beside the pot. If the pot can take on watching the heat and stirring, the waiting time could go to folding laundry, preparing for tomorrow, or simply sitting down for a moment.

The other point is the number of tools. Making a potage usually means a pot, a blender, a spatula, and more. The RSY-2 is designed to chop, heat, and stir in a single pot, so I can imagine fewer tools coming out and fewer kinds of things to wash. I haven’t measured how much easier it is or how much time it saves, so I won’t say that it definitely makes things easier.

Making it easier to add warm potage or soup as one more dish on a busy morning or a tired evening is the biggest appeal I see in this product.

Chopping, Heating, and Stirring Handled by Five Modes

The RSY-2 has five cooking categories.

  • SOYMILK
  • POTAGE&PASTE
  • SOUP&OKAYU
  • JUICE&CLEAN
  • WARM

The rated times in the official materials are 40 minutes for each of the three heated cooking modes, 3 minutes for JUICE&CLEAN, and 25 minutes for WARM. A rated time is not the same as the time from putting ingredients in to serving, so I would not assume it means the whole process, including preparation, gets shorter.

Choose a mode, and the unit works through chopping, heating, and stirring. Being able to leave heat control to the unit seems helpful both to people who are not used to cooking and to people who are tired.

What You Can Make: Menus the Official Materials Introduce

The official materials introduce potage, paste, soup, okayu (rice porridge), soy milk, juice, and similar items. For anything not listed there, it is safest to check the official guidance before trying it.

Separately from the SOYMILK mode, the official FAQ also gives a guideline for when you want to replace the milk in a recipe with soy milk: two parts soy milk to one part water. Before adapting a recipe, it is reassuring to check this guidance and the instruction manual.

The accessories are a cleaning brush, a measuring spoon, and a dedicated recipe book. At first, using the recipe book with the ingredients as written in the manual seems the safest approach.

What to Check Right Before You Buy

The 600ml Capacity

The RSY-2 has a capacity of 600ml. It measures about 16.5cm wide, 12.0cm deep, and 23.3cm high, weighs about 970g, and has a cord of about 1m. Its power consumption is listed as 600W, and 55W in JUICE&CLEAN mode.

Rather than turning 600ml into a fixed serving count, compare it with the bowl or cup you normally use and the amount you want to make at one time.

How It Differs from the Larger-Capacity RSY-3

The same series also has the RSY-3, a model with a larger capacity. According to the official page, the RSY-3 costs JPY 19,800 including tax, holds up to 800ml for hot dishes and up to 1,000ml for juice, weighs about 1.3kg, and has seven modes. It is also described with a “Taberu Soup” (hearty soup) mode, a remaining-time display, keep-warm/reheat, and IPX4-equivalent splash-resistant construction.

The RSY-2 does not have the “Taberu Soup” mode. When you make an RSY-3 recipe in the RSY-2, official support suggests reducing the ingredients to about two-thirds. The programs differ, though, so the result may not be the same.

My own view: if you prioritize the ease of a 600ml size for one person or a small household, the RSY-2 fits that picture. If you prioritize cooking for a family, a more substantial texture, and a larger capacity, the RSY-3 is the one to compare.

Care Rules

According to the official materials, the inside of the main unit can be washed, but the outside of the main unit and the power cord cannot be washed with water. The main unit cannot be fully submerged, and dishwashers and dish dryers cannot be used.

Dirt on the blades is normally cleaned with JUICE&CLEAN mode and the included cleaning brush. For stubborn dirt, soaking with citric acid or baking soda is described. Whether the washing-up feels lighter each time may depend on whether you find these rules a hassle.

Usage Limits

The instruction manual states that exceeding the Max line, using water at 40°C or higher, insufficient ingredients, and prohibited ingredients can cause the unit to stop or boil over. As a rule, prepare the ingredients as the manual describes.

It is made in China. It is not described as made in Japan.

Robu's Wrap-Up

I think the appeal of the RSY-2 lies in time you do not have to spend in front of the pot, and a kitchen scene where one pot is all you need. In exchange, there are the 600ml capacity, the care conditions, and the ingredient rules.

Before buying, check these five points.

  1. Is 600ml enough for the amount you want to make at one time?
  2. Do you have space to place and store it, at 16.5cm wide, 12.0cm deep, and 23.3cm high?
  3. Can you accept the care rules: no full submersion of the main unit, and no water washing of the outside or the power cord?
  4. Can you prepare the ingredients and amounts as the instruction manual describes?
  5. If you want to make a larger batch for a family, or want the ingredients to feel more substantial, have you compared it with the RSY-3?

As a tool that could make a warm bowl easier to add as one more dish, even on a tired day, the RSY-2 is one I am curious about. I hope you can compare it with the realities of your own kitchen and choose with confidence.
