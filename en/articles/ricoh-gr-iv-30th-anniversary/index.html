What Makes the RICOH GR IV 30th Anniversary Edition Special?

Ricoh Imaging has announced the RICOH GR IV 30th Anniversary Edition Kit, limited to 6,000 units worldwide. The limited number is appealing, but this is not a hands-on review. I do not have the camera, so the comparison below is based on Ricoh's official announcement and specifications.

Release date, quantity and price

Ricoh announced the kit on September 16, 2026, with release scheduled for October 21. Production is limited to 6,000 units worldwide. The manufacturer announcement lists an open price, while the Ricoh Imaging Store lists a tax-included price of JPY 259,800. The shared lottery for the official store and GR SPACE TOKYO accepted entries from noon on September 18 through 11:59 p.m. on September 24.

The camera is based on the RICOH GR IV, introduced in September 2025. This is not a new imaging system with a different sensor or lens. It is an anniversary package that distinguishes itself through finish and included accessories.

Six differences from the standard model

The confirmed differences are:

  1. Shutter and rear buttons colored to evoke the plated controls of the original GR1
  2. A special diamond-cut ring cap
  3. An anniversary power-off screen
  4. A genuine-leather finger strap with anniversary detailing
  5. A metal hot-shoe cover with anniversary detailing
  6. Sixteen pin badges: fourteen historical GR models plus anniversary and GR-logo designs

Ricoh says the diamond-cut ring cap is designed to catch the light when the lens is retracted while avoiding reflections on the subject during shooting. That makes it more than a purely decorative accent.

The core camera specifications are unchanged

The announcement states that the remaining camera specifications are the same as the standard product.

Lens 18.3mm, approximately 28mm equivalent, F2.8–F16

The anniversary version does not claim better image quality or stabilization than the standard GR IV. Buyers who prioritize photographic function can compare the two on price, finish and availability.

Travel-photography considerations

A roughly 28mm-equivalent view suits streets, architecture, food and people shown in their surroundings. At about 262g, the camera is also compact enough to carry during a long day of walking.

It is a fixed-lens camera, not an interchangeable-lens or zoom model. Travelers who often photograph distant subjects or want ultra-wide and telephoto views from one device should check whether that approach fits their needs. The roughly 250-shot battery figure is a test-based estimate, so longer days and video use may require a charging plan or a spare battery.

Who it may suit

It may appeal to people who:

  • Care about the history and design of the GR series
  • Prefer a 28mm-equivalent view for street and travel photography
  • Value the special finish and included accessories
  • Enjoy owning a limited object even when its photographic specifications are unchanged

The standard model may be the more practical comparison for people who care mainly about image quality, do not want the commemorative accessories, or prefer easier availability and a clearer street price.

These are specification-based considerations, not conclusions from hands-on use.

What remains unconfirmed

The primary sources reviewed here do not confirm:

  • Prices and stock at general retailers
  • Retailer allocation outside the official lottery
  • Whether individual cameras carry limited-edition numbers
  • Future resale prices

The official-store price and lottery method are confirmed, but general-retailer terms are not uniform. Buyers should compare price, stock, warranty and return conditions rather than act only on the limited quantity.

Robu’s Take

The anniversary model's value lies in its GR1-inspired finish, unique power-off graphic and commemorative accessories—not in extra photographic performance. If the standard GR IV already offers the image quality and portability you want, the decision comes down to whether the anniversary presentation is meaningful enough to justify its final retail price.

The official-store price and lottery conditions are now public, but general-retailer prices and availability can differ. The limited number is part of the appeal, but it should not replace a careful check of price, warranty and how the camera fits the way you actually photograph.
