Precision Machining, Traditional Coloring, Lacquer and Mother-of-Pearl: A Japanese Double-Edge Razor Made to Be Chosen

This is a product I cannot stop thinking about: the Tensei Oboro Nari double-edge razor from YUKYU JINROU.

Its copper handle carries deep blue and black tones reminiscent of sky seen through hazy clouds, topped by a precisely formed SUS316L stainless-steel head. The company behind it has spent decades making precision parts for semiconductor equipment and robots. It has now brought Japanese machining and traditional coloring into a tool that can be held every morning.

This feels like more than a tool for removing facial hair. It invites you to hold something beautiful, let its weight guide your hand, and shave with deliberate short strokes. That promise of turning morning grooming into a moment of composure is what caught my attention.

1. Japanese expertise, brought together in one small tool

The maker is Yamamoto Seiko, an Osaka metalworking company founded in 1955. It has produced precision parts for semiconductor manufacturing equipment, factory automation, industrial machinery and robots.

YUKYU JINROU is the company's first consumer brand. Its idea is to unite precision machining with traditional craft, repeatable accuracy with the expression of handwork, and carry those values into tools made for long use. Choosing a double-edge razor as its first product says a great deal about that philosophy.

Tensei Oboro Nari is not a display piece made merely to showcase technical ability. It puts that ability in your hand as part of an everyday routine. Precision machining that has supported industry is suddenly close enough to use in front of the mirror each morning. That closeness is compelling.

2. Copper and traditional coloring give every piece its own expression

Tensei Oboro Nari combines an SUS316L stainless-steel head with a copper body. It is turned and traditionally colored in Japan.

Precision turning creates an evenly proportioned cylinder. Traditional coloring then uses heat and chemical change to bring blue and black variation to the copper surface. A form shaped with mechanical accuracy gains the warmth and depth of handwork, bringing industrial design and craft together in one object.

The appearance changes with the light, and the hand-finished color and surface vary from piece to piece. Even two razors with the same name will not look exactly alike. The one that arrives in your hand is yours alone, and that individuality is part of the reason to choose it.

3. A simple double-edge design with practical replacement blades

A double-edge safety razor uses one blade in a clean, straightforward structure. It lets the user sense the distance between blade and skin and enjoy the smooth character of a single-blade shave. With fewer blades passing over the skin, it can also reduce the number of blade contacts during a stroke.

Tensei Oboro Nari includes five blades and accepts standard double-edge razor blades, so it is not limited to one proprietary system. YUKYU JINROU's own Japanese-made blades are sold in a 30-pack for ¥2,400, which works out to ¥80 per blade. The body can stay with you for years while only the thin blade is replaced.

With no unnecessary mechanism to hide it, the material, finish and feel in the hand become the character of the tool. The clarity of the double-edge format suits this expression of Japanese making particularly well.

4. Its 83 g weight encourages a deliberate shave

The razor measures 42 × 98 × 25 mm and weighs 83 g. It fits in the palm while retaining the reassuring weight of metal.

Rather than pressing, the recommended technique is to let the razor's weight do the work. Begin along the direction of hair growth and shave gradually with short strokes, sensing the angle of the blade and finding your own pace.

In that context, 83 g is more than a specification. It is the weight that allows your hand to relax and the tool to work. A task often rushed through in the morning can become a calm moment spent using a well-made object.

5. Five chapters invite you to choose a technique as well as a razor

The appeal of YUKYU JINROU extends beyond Tensei Oboro Nari. The official collection is organized into chapters built around different techniques and forms.

  • Sen no Sho pairs precise turning and balanced cylindrical forms with traditional coloring. It includes Tensei Oboro Nari, Ginsei Haruka Nari and Gansho Arawa Nari.
  • Kan no Sho cuts continuous patterns around the full cylinder and deepens their shadows with lacquer and smoked finishes. Hishi Kokuren and Asa Ryuren offer distinct patterns.
  • Tan no Sho celebrates the marks of traditional hammering, layered with traditional coloring. Shutsui brings the strength of handworked metal to the foreground.
  • Tsuya no Sho uses deep-cut patterns with lacquer, metal powder and mother-of-pearl to create shifting luster. Buyo Tenko Kokuyo and Buyo Tenko Ruri are the current expressions.
  • Miyabi no Sho is reserved for future ideas that may move beyond the existing categories.

If quiet blue-black depth is what draws you, Tensei Oboro Nari stands out. If patterns, hammer marks or the sheen of lacquer and mother-of-pearl speak more strongly, the other chapters are worth exploring. The choice is not only which razor to own, but which part of Japanese craft to bring into daily life.

6. Rinse, wipe and return it to a dry place

Care is straightforward. After use, rinse away shaving cream and hair with lukewarm water, wipe off moisture with a soft cloth, and return the razor to a dry place.

To preserve the traditional finish, handle the surface gently and avoid strong rubbing or abrasives. Replace the blade while continuing to use the body, and enjoy the way the metal's expression deepens over time. Care becomes part of making the tool your own.

7. ¥16,280 for Japanese craftsmanship you can use every morning

Tensei Oboro Nari is priced at ¥16,280 including tax.

That price brings together SUS316L and copper, precision turning, the blue-and-black depth of traditional coloring, and finishing performed one piece at a time. It also includes the individuality of each razor, the 83 g balance that shapes the shaving motion, and the pleasure of picking up a beautiful object every morning.

Grooming tools are used repeatedly. Choosing one that looks beautiful whenever you see it and lets you feel Japanese skill whenever you hold it gives the price a different meaning. I see ¥16,280 as the price of adding that richness to the start of each day.
