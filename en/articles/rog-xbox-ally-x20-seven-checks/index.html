Should You Buy the ROG XBOX Ally X20? Seven Checks Before Spending ¥258,800

Hi, I'm Roboo.

ASUS Japan released the 20th-anniversary ROG XBOX Ally X20 on 16 October 2026. The official ASUS Store price checked that day was ¥258,800 including tax.[1][4]

The specification sheet is the sort of thing that is enjoyable to read on its own: a 7.4-inch Full HD OLED display at 120Hz, AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme, 24GB of memory, a 1TB SSD and an 80Wh battery. But the same device also weighs about 756g and carries a price well into the ¥250,000 range.

I have not used the X20 myself. This is a buying guide based on the official information, organized around seven points that may give a buyer pause. Hands-on impressions belong to reviewers who have actually held the device; here, the aim is to separate what is already known from what still needs to be tested.

1. Price and release date

The standalone model is RC74XA-RZ2R24G1T, released on 16 October 2026. ASUS Store Japan listed it at ¥258,800 including tax when checked that day.[1][4]

A separate Bundle model combines the handheld with ROG XREAL R1 Edition 20 glasses, a Pelican protector case and dbrand accessories. If you are considering the standalone model, remember that those extras are not included.[2]

Retail prices move, so treat the amount above simply as the official store price displayed at the time of checking.

2. The conditions behind “1,400 nits” matter

The display is a 7.4-inch 1,920×1,080 OLED touchscreen with a 120Hz refresh rate. The product page also lists a 0.2ms response time, 30–120Hz variable refresh rate, VESA DisplayHDR True Black 1400, Dolby Vision and FreeSync Premium Pro.[2][3]

The eye-catching number is 1,400 nits, but the screen does not run at that brightness all the time. ASUS says Windows HDR must be enabled and that the maximum is used for bright highlights such as explosions or sunlight. It lists 600 nits for SDR and full-screen HDR content.[3]

The screen is clearly one of the X20's biggest attractions. Reflections, text legibility and eye comfort, including sensitivity to PWM, are personal, however. Those are details that a specification sheet cannot settle. If possible, change the brightness in a shop and look at the interface of a game you actually play.

3. Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme, 24GB and 1TB

The X20 uses an eight-core, 16-thread AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme, 24GB of LPDDR5X memory and a 1TB NVMe M.2 SSD. ASUS also advertises an NPU rated at up to 50 TOPS.[2][4]

TOPS is a prominent number, but it is not a game-frame-rate measurement. Actual performance depends on power mode, cooling, resolution, graphics settings, drivers and the game's own optimization. ASUS positions the system for AAA games, but this article contains no independent benchmark.

One terabyte sounds generous until several games larger than 100GB are installed. The system uses an M.2 2280 SSD and supports microSD cards, but replacement procedures, warranty terms and card performance are practical questions that may only arise after purchase.

4. TMR sticks and the adjustable D-pad are central features

The sticks use contactless TMR technology. ASUS claims improved accuracy and durability and says joystick power consumption is about 75 percent lower than on the preceding model. The D-pad can be lifted and rotated between layouts optimized for four-direction and eight-direction input. The ABXY buttons were also redesigned.[2]

Those are intriguing features for anyone who plays fighting, retro and action games on one device. Yet grip comfort, stick resistance, button noise and unintended D-pad inputs cannot be conveyed by text or numbers. With this model, holding it may tell you more than reading the processor list again.

5. Even an 80Wh battery does not translate into one runtime figure

The four-cell battery is rated at 80Wh, and the included USB-C adapter is rated at 68W. The top edge provides one USB4 Type-C port and one USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-C port; ASUS lists charging and display output for both.[4]

An 80Wh battery is large for a handheld, but runtime will still vary substantially with the game, brightness, 120Hz mode, wireless activity, graphics settings and power profile. The official specification reviewed for this article does not provide one universal play-time figure. If you plan to use it away from home, measurements from a similar game and power mode will be more useful than a single headline number.

6. “Portable” does not necessarily mean comfortable to hold indefinitely

ASUS lists dimensions of 300.0×121.0×27.5–51.3mm and a weight of approximately 756g.[4]

Grip shape and balance will affect comfort, but the 756g figure itself does not change. Some owners will use their lap or a table, add a stand and controller, or play in shorter sessions. A case, 68W charger and cable also make the bag heavier.

7. Windows flexibility comes with Windows maintenance

The device runs Windows 11 Home and uses XBOX mode and Armoury Crate SE to make controller-first navigation easier. ASUS emphasizes the ability to view games from multiple PC platforms in one place.[2][3]

That freedom has a cost. It remains a Windows PC, so operating-system updates, launcher accounts, drivers, security, text entry and per-game settings do not disappear. Nor is there a console-style guarantee that every title will behave under the same conditions.

A three-month Game Pass entitlement is advertised, but a valid payment method is required. Check the renewal terms during activation so the charge after the trial does not come as a surprise.[3][5]

Robu’s Take

The ROG XBOX Ally X20 makes more sense as a high-performance Windows PC that fits between two hands than as a conventional handheld console. Its 7.4-inch OLED panel, 120Hz, TMR sticks, 24GB of memory, 1TB SSD and 80Wh battery are all appealing. At the same time, the ¥258,800 price and approximately 756g weight remain at the center of the decision.

The anniversary design may be enjoyable, but it does not guarantee future value, so resale expectations should not be the reason to buy. Research the games you want to play, hold the device if possible and look closely at the screen. If you still want it in your hands after those steps, the price may finally make sense for you.
